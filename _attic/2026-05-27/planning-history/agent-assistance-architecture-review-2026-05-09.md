# Agent-Assistance Architecture Review — 2026-05-09

**Reviewer:** Independent (no prior session context)
**Artifacts:** `product/systems/agent-assistance.md`, `product/systems/member-self-record.md`, `product/systems/agent-assistance.md`, `planning/DECISIONS.md` ADR-6.

## Verdict

**Revise on three specific points before scenarios, then ship the b1 substrate.** The architecture is sound and load-bearing in all the right places — Delegation as a permission primitive, Self-Record as a Member-owned document distinct from the event log, Skills as composable capability bundles. The b1 commitment is genuinely small (tables, scope enum, two action handlers, two audit columns) and earns its keep by preventing a much larger retrofit later. But three things need work before scenarios are written: (1) `member_standing_signal` is not yet defined sharply enough to be implementable and overlaps `maker_signal` from ADR-3 in ways that will collide; (2) the privacy commitments in `member-self-record.md` are partially policy-shaped where they should be schema-shaped; (3) the action layer is named in ADR-6 as a consequence but is doing real architectural work that warrants its own ADR. Fix those three, drop two pieces of T2/T3 gold-plating, and the architecture is ready for the planning filter.

## Load-bearing — do not cut

1. **Delegation as a primitive, not a feature on the assistant** (`delegation.md` §"What Delegation Is"). The argument that the same machinery serves the assistant, Skills, and federation peers is correct and prevents three parallel permission systems from emerging. The two audit fields (`acting_member_id`, `via_delegation_id`) on every write are the load-bearing piece — without them at b1, every later agent feature is a retrofit.
2. **Confirmation-required scopes as a schema-level constraint, not a UX convention** (`delegation.md` T2 + ADR-6 Consequences). The line "the Member always presses publish" is the structural commitment that keeps the assistant from becoming the platform's voice. Stated as a constraint on the action layer, not a settings toggle. Keep this exact framing.
3. **The three-substrate separation: event log / Self-Record / scratchpad** (`member-self-record.md` §"The three layers"). Conflating any two of these is the failure mode the entire spec exists to prevent. The event log records what the Member did; the Self-Record records what they said about themselves; the scratchpad dies. This is the single most important architectural commitment in the three docs.
4. **Money-flow scopes do not exist** (`delegation.md` T2 + ADR-6). Categorical exclusion of monetary action from Delegation, encoded in the scope enum (no scope can be issued for it). This is the right place to make the commitment unrecoverable.
5. **Skills run in the Member's agent context, not on platform servers** (`skills.md` §"Skill execution model"). This is what makes the people-first commitment hold under composability — the platform cannot inadvertently become a data-aggregation layer because it never sees what Skills do with what they read. Critical commitment; structural, not policy.
6. **Self-Record export and purge ship at b1, even though no surface reads it** (`member-self-record.md` T1). This earns the privacy commitments before the data exists to violate them. Right call.

## Gold-plated — defer or cut

1. **Per-Skill Self-Record section scoping at T3** (`member-self-record.md` T3, `skills.md` T3). Real concern, but premature: there are no peer-authored Skills yet, no standing-tier Self-Records yet, and no way to know which sections cause friction in practice. Defer the design until after b2 ships and Members report what they actually want scoped.
2. **Skill bundles** (`skills.md` T3, "bakery starter pack"). Marketplace-shaped feature solving a problem (subscription friction) that won't exist until the catalog has hundreds of Skills. Cut from the spec; revisit if and when.
3. **Diff view + bulk re-confirm + Self-Record analytics** (`member-self-record.md` T3, multiple). All three are reasonable T3 surfaces individually; together they suggest the Self-Record is being designed as an admin tool. The b2 surface is the constraint that matters; T3 polish should be one open bullet, not three named surfaces. Slim T3 to "federation portability + per-Skill scoping" and drop the rest until evidence demands them.
4. **Granular scope reduction without revoke/regrant** (`delegation.md` T3). One-tap revoke + re-grant is fine for v1; the only reason to add mid-life shrinking is if revocation friction proves too high. No evidence yet. Defer.
5. **Skill execution analytics for the Member** (`skills.md` T3, `delegation.md` T3 same pattern). "47 drafts, 41 used" is a nice-to-have that requires aggressive event-log partitioning and a Member-facing analytics surface. Cut from T3 spec; the raw data will be there if Members ask.
6. **Federation Skills + federation Delegations as named T3 surfaces** (`skills.md` T3, `delegation.md` T3). The schema reservations are fine. The named user-facing surfaces are speculative — federation isn't designed yet (no `federation.md` exists). Defer the surface design language until federation has its own spec; keep only the schema reservations.

## Missing — gaps that will bite

1. **`member.md` does not exist in tree.** All three new specs cite it as authoritative ("per `member.md`"), and ADR-3 references `member.maker_signal` as canonical. This is a real referential gap — either create a stub now or change the references to ADR-3 directly. Otherwise the new specs sit on a phantom dependency.
2. **`member_standing_signal` definition is underspecified** (`member-self-record.md` §"Standing signal"). It's described as "mirror of `member.maker_signal`" but `maker_signal` itself is described in ADR-3 as deferred to a separate ticket. Two derived views where neither is defined creates a real risk of divergence — and the standing signal is doing more work (it gates Self-Record affordance prominence and assistant proposal aggressiveness, which are user-visible). Spec the inputs and the tier-cutoff thresholds before scenarios; don't ship two undefined views.
3. **No spec for what happens when a Skill misbehaves or is removed mid-subscription** (`skills.md` §"Skill execution model" mentions removal revokes Delegations atomically, but the Member-facing experience is undefined). What does the Member see? What if mid-task? What if the Skill produced drafts that haven't been published? This is the operational reality of distributed capability and needs at least a paragraph.
4. **Inferred-update evidence retention policy is missing** (`member-self-record.md` §"Append-only entry log" defines `evidence_event_ids` but says nothing about retention, deletion cascade, or what happens when those event-log entries themselves age out or are purged). If the Member purges their event log, what happens to inferred Self-Record entries that cite it?
5. **No threat model for Delegation token theft** (`delegation.md` §"Used by: the MCP server"). Every MCP tool invocation requires a Delegation token; the spec is silent on token rotation, token scope-narrowing, what happens on suspected compromise, and whether a Delegation can be issued with an IP/device pin. Federation T3 makes this acute; b1 schema reservations should at least name `revoked_at` semantics for compromise vs. expiry vs. user-initiated.
6. **Privacy commitments are partially policy where they should be schema** (`member-self-record.md` §"Privacy posture"). "Never trained on" and "never feed-fuel" are written as binding commitments but enforced by absence of code, not by a constraint. At b1, when the data doesn't exist, that's fine. At b2, the commitment needs a structural enforcement — e.g., a separate database role with no read access from the recommendation/feed services, codified in migrations. The spec should commit to this enforcement mechanism now, not at b2.

## Overconfident — soften or earn

1. **"Standing-derived persistence is structurally distinguishable from a Maker claim"** (ADR-6 + `member-self-record.md`). The standing signal is derived from "standing Items, recurrence depth, follower count, follow-throughs, publish frequency." A Member could trivially generate this pattern by posting recurring junk Items. Stated as resolved; actually a real gameability question. Soften from "structural commitment" to "current best-effort derivation, gameability monitored."
2. **"Never used as training data" as a binding commitment** (`member-self-record.md` §"Privacy posture"). Binding on whom? The platform's chosen LLM provider sees Self-Record content at inference time. The spec acknowledges this ("inference-time access by the Member's chosen LLM provider is unavoidable") but the headline "never trained on" is too absolute given that providers' training boundaries are governed by their TOS, not this platform's. Reframe as "the platform commits not to use Self-Record for training; provider commitments depend on the provider relationship the Member chooses."
3. **"Skill revenue does not flow through the platform — sustainable forever"** (`skills.md` §"Revenue posture", ADR-6). This is stated as a permanent commitment. It's a defensible posture today, but a Skills marketplace where sellers route around the platform's payment can also route around the platform's accountability mechanisms (refunds, dispute resolution, fraud). The commitment may hold; the certainty in the doc is unearned. Soften to "no platform cut at any tier currently planned" and acknowledge the open question of dispute mediation.
4. **"The b1 substrate is small"** (ADR-6 Consequences). The substrate as listed (three new tables, two audit columns, scope enum, export + purge actions, two derived views, event log additions across three systems, materialized-view-style refresh implications) is not small. It's defensible, but calling it small undersells the work and risks the planning filter approving it under false pretenses. Reframe as "small relative to the cost of retrofit; non-trivial to ship correctly."
5. **"Action layer is the single canonical write surface"** (ADR-6 Consequences). This is stated as a downstream consequence but it's actually a major architectural commitment — every existing write path needs to be refactored through it. It deserves its own ADR (call it ADR-7) with explicit consequences for the existing controllers in `web/`. Naming it as a consequence of ADR-6 risks the engineering work being deprioritized.
6. **Three update pathways for Self-Record will compose cleanly** (ADR-6, `member-self-record.md` T2). Explicit teach + confirmation-derived + inferred-and-proposed is a clean design on paper, but conflicts will be common in practice (the Member explicitly teaches something the assistant later infers a contradiction to; the inferred suggestion is rejected and re-proposed because the underlying behavior changes). Open question §"Inference cadence" gestures at this; the spec should treat composition as an open question, not a resolved one.

## Two questions I can't answer from the docs

1. **What's the structural answer to a peer- or community-authored Skill that defrauds Members financially off-platform?** The platform mediates manifest, subscription, and Delegation but explicitly does not touch payment. If the Sacramento Bakers' Guild publishes a paid Skill, takes payment, and never delivers — what's the platform's posture? "Member-level revoke is always available" doesn't address the money. This question changes whether Skills can credibly ship as a T3 with paid sources, or whether the paid-source surfaces should defer until a dispute mechanism exists.

2. **Does the canonical Cafe Capricho example actually exercise these specs cleanly?** Initiative is listed as Self-Record-relevant (current focus, pinned facts) and Skill-relevant (a CDFI loan-readiness Skill is the headline example). But Initiative itself defers to b2, and the agent surfaces don't ship until b2 either. Is the b1 substrate enough that when both ship together at b2, the integration is one ticket and not a re-architecture? I can't tell from the docs whether the schema reservations on the Item side compose with the schema reservations on the Delegation/Skill/Self-Record side without integration scenarios that don't exist yet. This question changes whether b2 is a coordinated double-ship or two independent rollouts.
