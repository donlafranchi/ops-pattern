# The Slow Economy Movement: Where We've Landed

## The Thesis

For the first time in American history, young people expect to be worse off than their parents. This is not a feeling — it's the visible result of an extractive economic structure that pulls wealth out of communities faster than communities can regenerate it. Wages stagnate, costs rise, ownership concentrates, and the local institutions that once sustained middle-class life (independent businesses, community banks, regional manufacturers, family farms) get hollowed out and replaced by extractive intermediaries that send the wealth elsewhere.

**The antidote is structural, not political.** It's a conscious redirection of consumer spending from extractive channels back into circulating ones. When money circulates locally, communities get wealthier together. When it leaves, they get poorer together. This is arithmetic, not ideology — the local multiplier effect is documented and measurable. Money spent at independent local businesses recirculates 3–4x in the local economy before leaving; money spent at chains recirculates roughly 1.4x.

The movement's name for this is the **Slow Economy**: slower, more deliberate, more rooted, and over time, more prosperous for the people doing it. It's not anti-capitalist. It's anti-extractive. It's pro-competition, pro-ownership, pro-community. It restores the conditions under which the American dream actually worked: that you could live a better life than your parents because the wealth your community generated stayed in your community.

It's slow because it has to be. There's no overnight switch. It's a gradual reweighting of where dollars go, made possible by visibility, coordination, and shared practice. That slowness is honest, and it's the source of its durability.

## The Path Forward

Three surfaces, one project. Each reinforces the others.

**1. The Practice — your dinner circle.** Find ten people in Sacramento who already make things or want to learn. Meet monthly. Make and trade goods you'd otherwise buy from a grocery store: bread, soap, ferments, candles, mended clothes, preserved food. Document recipes, format, lessons. This is the lived demonstration that the thesis works at human scale — proof of life. It also keeps you grounded. If you ever lose touch with what this is for, the circle reminds you.

**2. The Platform — the visibility and coordination layer.** A simple, focused tool that makes circulating spending findable and easy. Starts at farmers markets where vendors and intentional consumers already gather. Lets vendors get discovered between markets. Lets consumers follow vendors they meet, find them on off-days, and discover others like them. Over time, expands to other categories where the extraction-vs-circulation gap is large and the information is hidden — hotels, restaurants, services, household goods. The platform's job is to make the invisible math visible at the moment of decision.

**3. The Intelligence — the analytical layer that uses your actual skill.** Public-facing analysis of where money leaks, where it circulates, where opportunity exists for new local operators to fill needs. This is what makes the movement feel substantive rather than sentimental. It also creates the bridge to people who want to *start* something (aspiring small business owners), not just spend on something. Eventually published as reports, neighborhood maps, and "state of extraction" briefings that journalists, organizers, and ordinary people can use.

## The MVP — What to Build First

Resist building all three at once. The MVP is one focused thing that proves the central mechanic and creates the first wave of participants.

**Build a Sacramento-based "follow your vendors" web app, anchored at farmers markets.**

Core functionality, nothing more:
- Vendor profile: name, photo, what they sell, contact info, which markets they attend, social/web links
- Easy vendor sign-up via QR code at market booths (under 90 seconds to complete)
- Consumer-facing map and list of vendors filterable by market and category
- "Follow" function so consumers can save vendors they met and get notified when those vendors are at upcoming markets or have new offerings
- A short, prominent statement of the thesis on every page — not buried, not soft. The pitch lives in the product.

That's the v1. No payments. No e-commerce. No reviews. No accounts beyond what's needed to follow vendors. No scanner. No TikTok integration. No browser extension. Those can come later if the core mechanic works. Most won't be needed.

**The hard discipline: ship the rough version in 6–8 weeks, get it in front of one Sacramento market's worth of vendors and customers, and learn from real use before adding anything.**

## The Benefits, Stated Honestly

### For Sellers (vendors, makers, small operators)

- **Discoverability between markets.** Right now, customers who love what you make at Saturday's market have no easy way to find you on Wednesday. This closes that gap.
- **A growing audience of customers who already share your values.** Every consumer on this platform has self-selected into wanting to spend locally. You're not marketing to strangers; you're being introduced to people who are looking for you.
- **Free participation.** No fees for vendors at MVP stage. The movement does not extract from the people it's trying to support.
- **Eventually: market intelligence.** Down the road, vendors get insight into where demand exists in their region, what categories are under-served, and where to consider expanding their route.
- **Community belonging.** You're not just listed in a directory — you're part of a coherent, growing movement that gives your work cultural meaning beyond the transaction.

### For Buyers (consumers, community members)

- **A way to actually do the thing you already believe in.** Most people who care about local spending do it inconsistently because the friction is high and the information is scarce. This makes it easy.
- **Stay connected to the makers you discover.** No more "I bought amazing jam at the market and forgot the vendor's name." You can follow, find, and return to the people whose work you value.
- **Visible impact.** Over time, see how your spending shifts and what that shift means for your community — not as a guilt trip, but as a signal that what you're doing matters.
- **Discovery of new local options.** The platform surfaces vendors and makers you didn't know existed in your area, expanding your options for things you're already buying.
- **Membership in something larger.** You're not a customer. You're part of a community-wide effort to take back economic power and rebuild the conditions under which the American dream actually works.

### For the Community

- **Money retained locally.** Every dollar redirected from extractive channels to circulating ones stays in the community 2–3x longer. Multiplied across thousands of households, this is the difference between a hollowing-out neighborhood and a thriving one.
- **More viable small businesses.** As more dollars circulate, more local operators reach viability, which means more jobs, more diversity of offerings, more resilience.
- **A path forward for the next generation.** The honest answer to "why will my kids have it worse than me" is that the current system extracts faster than communities can regenerate. The honest answer to *how to fix it* is to slow the extraction and accelerate the regeneration. This movement is one component of that — not the whole answer, but a real component that ordinary people can participate in without waiting for political change.

## What This Movement Will and Won't Do

**It will:** Slowly, measurably, redirect a portion of consumer spending from extractive to circulating channels. Make local options more findable. Give participants a sense of agency and community in an economy that mostly takes those away. Generate stories, data, and language that influence how people think about where their money goes. Create a replicable model that other communities can adopt.

**It won't:** Defeat Amazon. Reverse 40 years of consolidation overnight. Make every participant rich. Provide a direct, traceable line between any individual purchase and a personal benefit. Be politically uncontroversial as it grows.

**The honest pitch to participants is:** *Your individual contribution is small. The collective contribution, over time, is enormous. The benefits are slow and partly invisible. But the alternative is to keep funding the system that's squeezing you. Doing this together, even imperfectly, is how we change direction. Slowly is the only honest speed for this kind of work.*

## What "Restoring the American Dream" Actually Means Here

The American dream wasn't a promise of wealth. It was a promise that ordinary work, in an ordinary community, could produce a life better than the one before. That promise broke because the conditions that made it work — local ownership, regional self-sufficiency, dispersed economic power — got dismantled in favor of a system that produces cheaper goods but extracts community wealth as the cost.

You can't restore the dream by demanding it back. You can restore it by rebuilding the conditions that made it possible: local businesses that recirculate revenue, community institutions that retain wealth, and consumers who understand that their spending is, collectively, the most powerful economic vote they have left.

This movement is a small but real step in that direction. Done well, it gives the next generation a few more options, a few more thriving local businesses to work for or buy from, a few more dollars staying in the community. Done at scale across many communities, it begins to shift the structural conditions themselves.

That's the work. It's slow. It's worth it.
