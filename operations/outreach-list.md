# Maker & Service Provider Outreach List

Categories of people to seek out as founding participants on the Project — beyond farmers market vendors. These are the people who make "follow your makers" a genuinely broader platform rather than a market-day app.

## How to Use This List

Aim for **breadth early**, not depth. Getting three people from each of ten categories is more valuable than thirty bakers, because it demonstrates the platform is for *makers broadly*, not just food. Prioritize people who already have some presence (Instagram, Etsy, a card at the coffee shop) — they've already shown they want to be found. They're not who you're converting; they're who you're consolidating.

---

## Food Makers (beyond market stalls)

- Home bakers — sourdough, cookies, pastries, tortillas
- Fermenters — kimchi, sauerkraut, hot sauce, kombucha, miso
- Preservers — jam, pickles, canned goods, dried herbs
- Specialty producers — honey, maple syrup, vinegar, salt blends
- Home coffee roasters
- Pasta makers
- Tamale and empanada makers (often informal, highly local)
- Backyard egg producers
- Microgreens and sprout growers
- Mushroom cultivators

**Where to find them:** Instagram hashtags by neighborhood, cottage food permit registries (public in CA), local food co-op bulletin boards, farmers market side stalls.

---

## Textile and Fiber Makers

- Seamstresses and tailors (especially mending and alteration specialists)
- Knitters and crocheters selling finished goods
- Weavers and spinners
- Quilters
- Dyers (natural and botanical dyes)
- Leatherworkers (bags, belts, wallets, repair)
- Shoemakers and cobblers

**Where to find them:** Local yarn stores, Etsy shops filtered by location, fiber arts guilds.

---

## Wood and Metal Makers

- Furniture makers and woodworkers
- Cutting board and kitchen goods makers
- Knife makers and bladesmiths
- Blacksmiths (architectural, decorative, tool)
- Jewelers (small-batch, not mass-production)
- Metal fabricators doing small commission work

**Where to find them:** Maker spaces, community college craft programs, Instagram, local craft fairs outside the farmers market circuit.

- Soap makers

- Broom makers

## Repair and Restoration Services

This category is especially important — it directly supports the "buy once, buy well, repair before replacing" philosophy.

- Bicycle mechanics (independent, not chain shops)
- Appliance repair (small operators who fix rather than replace)
- Electronics and phone repair shops
- Shoe and leather repair
- Furniture restoration and refinishing
- Watch and clock repair
- Camera repair
- Musical instrument repair (guitars, pianos, brass)
- Pottery and ceramics repair (kintsugi practitioners)
- Knife sharpening (often mobile or weekend-only)
- Seamstress/tailor (mending specifically)
- Lamp rewiring
- Typewriter and mechanical restoration

**Where to find them:** Yelp filtered for "family owned," older business directories, craigslist services section, referrals from hardware stores.

## Sacramento-Specific Starting Points

Since Sacramento is the MVP launch city, concrete places to walk into and talk to people:

- **Oak Park Farmers Market, Midtown Farmers Market, Sunday Sac Co-op Market** — primary vendor recruitment
- **Sacramento Natural Foods Co-op** — their bulletin board and producer list
- **Display California, Guild Theater neighborhoods** — dense with small independent shops
- **Verge Center for the Arts** — maker community hub
- **Curtis Park Village, East Sac, Oak Park, Land Park** — neighborhoods with active maker/homesteader populations
- **Hacker Lab Sacramento** — overlaps with maker community
- **Sacramento Urban Farms Coalition** — direct line to urban growers
- **18th & J / R Street corridor** — independent service businesses

## Approach Script (Short)

"I'm building something for people like you in Sacramento. It's a simple way for folks who want to spend locally to find and follow the people who actually make what they use. It's free to be listed, there's no fees, and you keep all your customer relationships. We're starting with 50 or so Sacramento makers and growers — I'd love to include you. Takes about 90 seconds to sign up."

## Recruitment Sequencing

1. **Weeks 1–4:** Farmers market vendors (the density wedge). Get 30–50 profiles live.
2. **Weeks 4–8:** Food makers beyond markets (home bakers, fermenters, preservers). These people overlap socially with market vendors, so recruit via referral.
3. **Weeks 8–12:** Repair and restoration trades. This opens the "Slow Economy" framing beyond food and is essential for the platform's identity.
4. **Weeks 12+:** Textile, wood, home goods makers, and the Neighbors layer rollout.

---

**Priority rule of thumb:** For every category, ask — would the person listed here show up to a neighborhood skill-share potluck? If yes, they're the right kind of first user. If no, they're a later-stage user.
