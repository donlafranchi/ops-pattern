Operating Model v2

As of 2026-09-30 · Member-owned cooperative structure, non-voting economic shares, redistributive commerce cut, dual-mandate board

What changed from v1: The member layer is no longer a sibling entity — it is now the owner of the platform via a member cooperative holding majority voting control. Employees and any outside capital receive non-voting economic interests. The zero-fee commitment on member commerce is replaced by a small commerce cut whose excess flows back to seller communities. The board's dual mandate is named explicitly in the charter.

Platform_Goals Sections 4 and 7 still need to be formally revised to match.

The critical decoupling: economic upside and governance are separate instruments. Nobody can accumulate ownership to accumulate control. Nobody can accumulate control to extract wealth.

3. Commerce cut & community flow-through

1

Small commerce fee on member-to-member transactions

Working answer: 2–3% flat. Collected at time of transaction by the platform.

2

Platform PBC keeps only what it needs to operate

Cap set at operating cost + defined buffer (e.g., 1.2x). This funds engineering, hosting, verification, member support, and reserves.

3

Excess flows to the seller's community grant fund

Every seller is associated with a home community (location primitive). The excess portion of the fee on their sales credits that community's grant fund.

4

Cross-community and edge cases roll up to the national fund

When a seller has no clear home community (federated instance, roaming vendor, national brand doing local), the excess routes to the National Grant Fund, which supports cross-community initiatives — infrastructure, replicable playbooks, larger commons investments.

5

Local grant funds are governed by local members

Each community's fund is spent by that community's members via a light governance mechanism (proposal + vote in the cooperative). The National Fund is governed by the co-op board.

Story test: "Every purchase you make from a local seller sends a few cents back to their community's fund. Nothing gets extracted to shareholders — because there are none." That framing has to hold up under scrutiny; the structure above is designed to make it true.

4. The dual-mandate board

The Platform PBC board is charter-bound to two co-equal mandates. Neither dominates by default; both must be reported on annually and both must be considered in every material decision.

The two mandates

  • Sustainability mandate: the platform must remain financially viable and operationally sound in perpetuity. Reserves, prudent risk management, competitive position, protocol maintenance.
  • Community mandate: the named community metrics must trend in the right direction. If any trends wrong for two consecutive reporting periods, the board must publish a corrective plan.

When they conflict — the tie-breaker

  • Charter defines a rebuttable presumption in favor of the community mandate. The board can override in the interest of sustainability, but must document the rationale publicly.
  • Over three consecutive periods of sustainability-favored decisions, the member cooperative gains a formal review right on the next material decision.

Legal structure warning: one-member-one-vote is not native to Delaware PBC form — voting rights normally follow shares. Options for making this work: (a) Delaware PBC where the majority voting shares are held by a cooperative corporation formed under a state statute that supports one-member-one-vote (Wisconsin, Massachusetts, others), (b) an LLC operating agreement that customizes voting membership units separately from economic units, (c) a hybrid where the platform is a PBC but member governance runs through a separate association with contractual control rights. This is a real fork and needs specialized counsel — the answer isn't obvious and each path has different implications for capital, employees, and federation.
