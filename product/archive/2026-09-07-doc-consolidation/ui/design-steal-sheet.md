# Design Steal Sheet

> Picked for what's worth stealing, not for category fit. Organized by what each one teaches.

## On-thesis (community around things, place, real identity)

**Letterboxd** — Community-around-items done right. People are real, "likes" function as endorsements, conversation lives on the film page (an item), not on neighborhoods. Closest spiritual cousin to what we're building. **Steal:** Item page as the social surface. Identity-floor without friction. Endorsements that feel like taste, not judgment.

**Strava** — Kudos = endorsements. Place-based, identity-floor, calm. **Steal:** How segments make a location a first-class object. Endorsement as a single-tap gesture that carries social weight without stars or reviews.

**Are.na** — Anti-algorithm, collection-based, quiet. **Steal:** The calm-feed proof that quiet works. No engagement bait, no red badges, no infinite scroll — and people still come back.

**Komoot** — Locality made beautiful. Maps as primary surface without feeling like a map app. **Steal:** How to make place feel alive in the UI without the map dominating.

## Craft bar (the "this feels expensive" reference)

**Linear** — Current gold standard for product UI. Typography, spacing, motion, keyboard-first. **Steal:** Restraint. Density without clutter. How to make a tool feel fast and considered at the same time.

**Things 3** — Microinteraction canon. **Steal:** The choreography of small actions — checking off, adding, reordering. Motion that confirms without decorating.

**Arc Browser** — Distinctive identity without being weird for its own sake. **Steal:** How a product earns the right to have personality. The oak mark needs this same confidence.

## Warmth + voice

**Headspace** — Warmth via illustration and tone, never saccharine. **Steal:** How to be warm without being soft. The thesis page and onboarding need this register.

**Duolingo** — Character system done with taste. Probably too playful for us, but worth studying. **Steal:** How motion and personality coexist with utility. The acorn lifecycle could learn from this.

## Typography + reader-respect

**Substack** — Reader-first hierarchy, content as the design. **Steal:** How to let the thing being shown carry the screen. The feed and item pages should feel this way — chrome disappears, content speaks.

**Stripe (docs + marketing)** — The web typography reference. **Steal:** Information hierarchy, code/content rhythm. The thesis page and any long-form surface should hit this bar.

## Anchor references

If forced to pick three to design against daily:

1. **Letterboxd** — the model for community-around-items without becoming a review hellscape
2. **Linear** — the craft bar
3. **Are.na** — the calm-feed proof that quiet works

Plus **Strava** as a fourth when endorsements become a design problem (b2).
