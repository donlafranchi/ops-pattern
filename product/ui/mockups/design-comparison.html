Gap Analysis: Main Street DLS vs Airbnb

Both mockups show the same six items — three products with photos, one event, one service, and one idea (the latter three without photos). The comparison isolates exactly where our DLS diverges from the level of visual polish Airbnb achieves.

Dimension Airbnb Main Street DLS Verdict
Background color Pure white (#FFFFFF). Every surface is white. The cards float visually because photography does the separation work. Warm off-white Canvas (#FAFAF7). Slightly warmer. Cards on white would create a subtle warm-cool contrast that can read as muddy at a glance. Close call
Typography weight distribution Cereal Bold (700) for card titles. One weight dominates the card. High title contrast makes scanning fast. Inter Medium (500) for card titles per the DLS card recipe ("Title 16px/500"). Reads lighter, less scannable at speed. The 600 and 700 weights exist in the scale but aren't assigned to cards. Gap — bump card title to 600
Card title size 15px semibold. Compact but punchy because of the weight. 16px medium. Slightly larger but softer. Net effect: titles have less visual authority despite being bigger. Gap — weight matters more than size
Social proof (ratings) Star + numeric rating on every card. Top-right of info block. Drives trust at a glance. No ratings (per principles.md — no reviews). Replaced by ownership tier badge and kind label. The badge is useful but doesn't serve the same "is this good?" signal. Intentional trade-off
No-photo card treatment Light gray (#f7f7f7) background with generous padding, a thin red accent line, uppercase category label, large title, and secondary meta. Reads as editorial / magazine layout. Content-rich, not empty. White background with hairline border, pistachio-tinted kind badge, decorative emoji circle, title + meta. Similar approach but the emoji circle is informal, and the pistachio badge introduces color that Airbnb avoids. Slightly busier. Close — refine badge restraint
Card spacing (feed) 20px horizontal padding, 24px between cards. Tight vertical rhythm, generous side margins. 24px horizontal padding (per DLS "24px page gutters mobile"), 24px between cards. Slightly wider margins mean less image width. At 375px viewport, that's 327px vs 335px image width — small but visible. Match
Image aspect ratio 1:1 square for the mobile feed. Maximizes visual impact in a vertical scroll. 1:1 square (matching). The DLS says "3:2 or 1:1" — 1:1 is the right choice for mobile feed. Match
Heart/save icon White stroke, dark semi-transparent fill, shadow. High visibility on any photo. Same treatment on photo cards. On text-forward cards, switches to muted stroke with no fill — appropriate but less discoverable. Match
Filter pills 1px border, white bg, dark text. Active: inverted (dark bg, white text). Below search bar, separated by a border-bottom hairline. Same visual logic (DLS pill/chip recipe matches). Active state uses --color-fg (Oak Green) instead of pure black. Reads slightly softer but functionally identical. Match
Search bar White pill, subtle shadow, two text lines (primary bold + secondary muted), filter icon on right. Top of feed. Inviting but doesn't dominate. Same structure, same shadow treatment. DLS search bar recipe is a direct Airbnb analog. Missing: the right-side filter button (not in DLS spec). Close — add filter affordance
Accent color restraint Rausch Red appears only on: the bottom-nav active icon, the "Guest favorite" badge, and the thin accent line on text-forward cards. Three instances in the entire feed. Everything else is black, white, gray. Pistachio appears on: ownership tier badges (2 cards), kind badge on text-forward cards (3 cards), kind label text below card info (all cards), decorative emoji circle bg. That's 8+ instances. The green is more diffuse. Gap — reduce accent frequency
Information density per card Title, subtitle, one detail line, price. Four lines max. No labels, no badges below the image. Title, subtitle, kind label, price. Also four lines — but the kind label is an extra semantic layer Airbnb doesn't need (they only have one content type). When the kind label is removed for a homogeneous feed, density matches. Close — kind label adds necessary info
Color temperature Cool-neutral. Pure white + #222 text + gray secondary. No warmth anywhere. The photography carries all warmth. Warm-neutral. Off-white canvas + Oak Green text + warm muted gray. The warmth is pleasant but means the chrome competes slightly more with photography for emotional register. Intentional — earthy palette
Bottom nav White bar, hairline top border, icon + label, active state in Rausch Red. Same structure. Active state in Oak Green (--color-fg) instead of an accent. Reads more muted — the active tab doesn't pop as hard. Gap — active nav needs more contrast

Top 5 Actionable Gaps

1. Card title weight (500 → 600). The single highest-leverage change. Airbnb's titles scan faster because they're bolder. Bumping the card recipe from 500 to 600 (SemiBold) — already in the Inter family — would close the biggest visual-authority gap without touching the type scale.

2. Accent color frequency. Airbnb uses Rausch Red three times in the entire feed; we use pistachio eight-plus times. The ownership tier badge on photos is high-value (unique to our platform). The kind label below the card info and the kind badge on text-forward cards are lower-value — consider dropping the kind label to a muted gray, and making the text-forward badge monochrome (dark text on light gray, like Airbnb's category labels).

3. Active bottom-nav indicator. Oak Green on white is low contrast for a nav highlight. Two fixes: use --pistachio-700 or --pistachio-800 for the active icon, or add a 2px accent-colored top border on the active tab. Airbnb uses their loudest color here intentionally.

4. Search bar filter affordance. Airbnb's right-side filter icon (circle with sliders) is a high-utility touch for a multi-type marketplace. The DLS search bar recipe doesn't include it. Adding a bordered circle icon on the right of the search pill would be a clean addition.

5. Text-forward card refinement. The decorative emoji circle works as a placeholder but will look informal at scale. Consider replacing it with a subtle SVG illustration per kind (calendar icon for events, tools icon for services, lightbulb for ideas) rendered at low opacity in the upper-right — same position, more polished. The Airbnb approach (thin accent line + uppercase category) is cleaner; we could adopt the line treatment using a pistachio-700 hairline instead of a full badge.

What the DLS Gets Right

The fundamentals are solid. Card spacing, image treatment (borderless, radiused), search bar structure, pill/chip behavior, shadow restraint (hairlines over shadows), and the overall visual hierarchy all track Airbnb's patterns closely. The warm-neutral palette is a deliberate differentiation, not a gap — it gives the feed an earthy, grounded quality that pure-white Airbnb lacks. The ownership tier badges are genuinely valuable UI that Airbnb has no analog for. The DLS is 80% there; the remaining 20% is weight, accent discipline, and small chrome refinements.
