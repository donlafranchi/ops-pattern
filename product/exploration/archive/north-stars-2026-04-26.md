# North Stars

The litmus tests for every feature. If a piece of work doesn't serve at least one of these, escalate before building.

## The Five

**1. Producers get found, get paid, grow.**
Existing makers, growers, ranchers, bakers, hosts, drivers, anyone who sells what they produce. The platform is their marketing department. Every feature should give them leverage they didn't have — followers they can reach, intelligence about what's working, tools to compete with bigger players who have a marketing department and a data team.

**2. Buyers find local options and stick with them.**
Discovery that's honest. Ranking that reflects proximity and quality, not ad spend. The ability to follow makers and hear from them between visits, so a one-time encounter at a market becomes an ongoing relationship.

**3. The onramp to producerhood is open.**
Someone who wants to make money from what they make, grow, cook, or know how to do should find this platform easier than driving for a delivery app. Lightweight registration. Education from producers already here. Tools that scale from "I sell jam to neighbors" to "I run a small business."

**4. Communities can organize and signal demand.**
The community itself should be able to surface what's missing, vet new business ideas before someone bets on them, and pool labor or capital when a project is bigger than one person. Demand signaling, group buying, shared infrastructure projects — the platform is the coordination layer.

**5. Wealth circulates locally.**
Every product decision is weighed against whether it keeps money in the community or sends it elsewhere. Fees flow back into the communities that generate them. Local commerce is the default; cross-community commerce is welcome but visible.

## How They Relate

Loops 1, 2, and 3 are the marketplace mechanics — what the app does day to day. Loop 4 is the community layer that makes the marketplace into a movement. Loop 5 is the outcome we measure.

If a feature does not advance at least one of these, it does not serve the mission. See [mission.md](mission.md) for the broader arc and [platform-promise.md § Our Promise to Producers](platform-promise.md) for how loop 1 is operationalized.

## The Wedge

We start at farmers markets because that's where producers and buyers already gather and trust each other. The model proves out there, then expands — first to broader market categories (not just produce), then to off-market producers, then to services, lodging, rides, and eventually any local business a community needs. Each step earns the right to the next.

Food-first decisions in b1 and b2 are wedge tactics, not mission scope. Nothing we build should prevent the broader expansion. See [mission.md § The Arc](mission.md).
