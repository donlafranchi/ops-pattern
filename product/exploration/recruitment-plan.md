# SocialUs — Recruitment Plan

> A solo founder's playbook for recruiting the right people at the right time, when AI agents handle most of the coding and the platform is pre-revenue.

## The Situation

SocialUs is a coordination platform for collective action in local communities. Farmers markets are the wedge; the vision is broader. One founder. AI agents (Claude, GPT, etc.) handle 80–90% of the coding. The platform is at MVP stage, pre-revenue, with no outside funding.

The founder needs people — but not the way a typical startup does. The AI stack covers engineering throughput longer than it covers go-to-market, community trust, legal exposure, and design taste. The bottleneck above first traction is buyer trust, not code output.

**What this plan is not:** a headcount plan. This is a sequenced strategy for recruiting expertise through advisors, fractional contributors, mission-aligned volunteers, and eventually full hires — matched to what a people-first, pro-competition platform actually needs.

---

## Part 1: What the AI Stack Doesn't Cover

Research (Foundra, Founder Institute, Carta Solo Founders Report 2025) converges on a clear pattern: when AI handles coding, the solo founder ceiling is no longer engineering — it's commercial traction, legal exposure, design quality, and community trust. The human expertise gaps that matter most for SocialUs:

**Judgment and taste gaps** — things AI can approximate but can't own:
- **Design taste and brand identity.** AI generates competent UI. It does not generate a cohesive visual identity that makes people trust a community platform on first contact. The difference between "looks fine" and "I want to be part of this" is a human designer's eye.
- **Community strategy and seeding.** AI can draft outreach copy. It cannot show up at a farmers market, earn a vendor's trust, run a beta group, or navigate the politics of a market manager association. This is handshake work.
- **Legal and compliance.** Community platforms carry real liability — content moderation, trust & safety policy, terms of service, data privacy (CCPA/GDPR), payment compliance. Getting this wrong early creates existential risk. AI can draft; a lawyer must review and own.
- **Go-to-market storytelling.** The founder can articulate the vision. But translating "coordination layer for collective action" into copy that makes a Sacramento baker sign up requires marketing craft and local knowledge.

**Trust gaps** — things that require a human face:
- **Partnerships and business development.** Farmers market associations, local chambers of commerce, city economic development offices, food policy councils — these relationships are built in person by people with existing networks.
- **Trust & safety.** Even at small scale, a community platform needs policy for content, disputes, and harmful behavior. Early-stage companies typically handle this ad hoc until it blows up. SocialUs should get ahead of it with at least advisory-level expertise.

**Amplification gaps** — things where one person's network isn't enough:
- **Local organizers and community champions.** Farmigo (a local food platform) measured success not by users but by organizers — the volunteers who build communities on the platform. SocialUs needs the same: real people in real places who adopt the platform and recruit their neighbors.

## Part 2: Priority Roles — Sequenced

### Now (MVP → First Users)

These roles unblock the transition from "working product" to "product people actually use."

**1. Community Lead / First Local Organizer (Sacramento)**
- *What they do:* Recruit the first 10–20 producers and 3–5 organizers in West Sacramento / East Sacramento. Attend farmers markets. Demo the platform. Collect feedback. Run the first beta community.
- *Who this is:* Someone already embedded in the Sacramento local food / small business scene. Knows vendors by name. Might be a market manager, a food co-op volunteer, a local food blogger, or a small business advocate.
- *Compensation:* Equity (0.5–1%) + title (Community Director / Head of Community) + the chance to shape something from day one. No cash initially; transition to stipend or part-time pay at first revenue.
- *Where to find them:* Sacramento Natural Foods Co-op, Sacramento Area Farmers Market Association, local food policy councils, West Sacramento Main Street program, Oak Park neighborhood groups. Personal outreach, not job boards.

**2. Designer (Fractional / Project-Based)**
- *What they do:* Create the visual identity, brand system, and design language that makes SocialUs feel trustworthy and alive — not like a generic startup. Review and elevate AI-generated UI. Establish the design tokens, type scale, color system, and component patterns that the AI stack then applies consistently.
- *Who this is:* A product designer or brand designer with community/marketplace experience. Someone who's designed for trust — fintech, healthcare, or community platforms.
- *Compensation:* Project fee ($3K–$8K for initial brand + design system) or fractional rate ($100–$200/hr, 5–10 hrs/month ongoing). Equity sweetener (0.25–0.5%) for ongoing commitment.
- *Where to find them:* Dribbble, Behance, ADPList, local AIGA chapter, design Twitter/Threads. Mission-driven designers on Purpose Jobs. Referrals from other startup founders.

**3. Legal Advisor**
- *What they do:* Review ToS, privacy policy, and content moderation framework. Advise on platform liability, marketplace compliance, and eventual payment-rail requirements. Available for 1–2 calls/month.
- *Who this is:* A startup attorney or tech-focused generalist who's touched internet platform law, marketplace liability, and data privacy. Ideally has worked with community platforms or marketplaces.
- *Compensation:* FAST agreement, 0.25–0.5% equity, 2-year vest. Possibly a small retainer ($500–$1K/month) once revenue starts.
- *Where to find them:* Sacramento / Bay Area startup legal community. LegalZoom alternatives: Clerky, Stripe Atlas network, YC's free legal resources. Local bar association startup law sections. Warm intros from other founders.

### Soon (First Users → Product-Market Fit)

These roles matter once there are real users generating real feedback.

**4. Go-to-Market / Growth Advisor**
- *What they do:* Help the founder define the acquisition funnel for producers and members. Advise on positioning, messaging, channel strategy. Not execution — strategy and pattern recognition from someone who's done it.
- *Who this is:* A founder or marketing leader who's grown a marketplace or community platform from 0 to 10K users. Ideally someone with local/SMB GTM experience, not enterprise SaaS.
- *Compensation:* Advisory board, FAST agreement, 0.25–0.5% equity. Monthly or biweekly calls.
- *Where to find them:* On Deck, Reforge alumni, GrowthMentor, First Round Capital's network, Lenny's Newsletter community. Founders of adjacent platforms (Nextdoor alumni, Yelp early team, local marketplace founders).

**5. Trust & Safety Advisor**
- *What they do:* Help design the content moderation framework, dispute resolution process, and reporting system before the first incident. Set policy foundations.
- *Who this is:* Someone with T&S experience at a community platform (Nextdoor, Facebook Groups, Discord, Reddit, Airbnb). Even 2–3 years of experience is enough at this stage.
- *Compensation:* Advisory, 0.1–0.25% equity. Quarterly check-ins, on-call for incidents.
- *Where to find them:* Trust & Safety Professional Association (TSPA), All Tech Is Human community, T&S Network starter kit community. LinkedIn outreach to mid-level T&S professionals at community platforms.

**6. Additional Local Organizers (2–3 new markets)**
- *What they do:* Replicate what the first Community Lead did in Sacramento — recruit producers and members in 2–3 adjacent markets (Davis, Elk Grove, Folsom, or wherever traction suggests).
- *Who this is:* Same profile as #1 — embedded in the local community, knows vendors, trusted by neighbors.
- *Compensation:* Volunteer with recognition (featured on the platform, "Founding Organizer" status, early access to tools) + small equity grants (0.1–0.25% each) for those who commit ongoing.
- *Where to find them:* The first organizer's network. Sacramento Region Community Foundation. UC Davis food systems program. Local Rotary/Lions clubs.

### Later (Product-Market Fit → Scale)

These become real hires when there's revenue or funding.

**7. Head of Partnerships / Business Development**
- Full-time. Owns relationships with farmers market associations, local government economic development, food policy organizations, small business associations at regional and eventually national level.

**8. Full-Stack Marketer**
- Full-time. Owns content, social, email, and local PR. Translates the vision into stories that make local producers and members sign up.

**9. Operations / Customer Success**
- Full-time. Onboards producers, handles support, manages the growing organizer network.

**10. Engineering Lead (Human)**
- The first human engineer. Not to replace AI agents but to own architecture decisions, review AI-generated code for security and quality, manage deployments, and handle the 10–20% of work that requires human judgment (infrastructure, security hardening, payment integration, data migrations).

## Part 3: Compensation Models That Work Pre-Revenue

### The FAST Agreement — Default for Advisors

The Founder Institute's FAST (Founder/Advisor Standard Agreement) is the standard. Version 3 (July 2026) is a 5-page document that covers equity, vesting, engagement level, and company stage. Use it for every advisor relationship.

| Engagement Level | Pre-Seed Equity | Vesting | Cliff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard (monthly calls, intros) | 0.25% | 2 years | 3 months |
| Strategic (monthly + recruiting + customer calls) | 0.5% | 2 years | 3 months |
| Expert (weekly, deep involvement) | 1.0% | 2 years | 3 months |

### Equity Budget — Guard the Pool

Total advisor/early-contributor equity should not exceed 5% of the company. The ranges below are targets — not all roles will land at the high end simultaneously. Allocate roughly:

- Community Lead / first organizer: 0.5–1.0%
- Designer (if ongoing): 0.25–0.5%
- Legal advisor: 0.25%
- GTM advisor: 0.25–0.5%
- T&S advisor: 0.1–0.25%
- Local organizers (pool for 5–10 people): 1.0% total
- Reserve for future advisors/early hires: 1.0–1.5%

Target total: 3.5–5.0%. If any single allocation lands at the high end, trim others or draw from the reserve.

### Non-Equity Compensation

For community organizers and contributors who aren't advisors:

- **Founding status.** "Founding Organizer" or "Founding Producer" designation — permanent, visible on their profile. This matters to mission-driven people.
- **Platform prominence.** Featured placement, early access to new features, input on product direction.
- **Skill building.** Access to the founder, exposure to startup building, reference/recommendation for their own careers.
- **Revenue share (future).** When the platform generates revenue, organizers who drove adoption in their market get a share. Define the formula now even if the dollars are zero.
- **Stipends.** Small monthly stipends ($200–$500) once any revenue exists, for organizers who commit ongoing hours.

### Fractional / Project Compensation

For the designer and other project-based contributors:

- **Hybrid model.** 50% cash / 50% equity-equivalent. A $6K brand project becomes $3K cash + 0.25% equity.
- **Deferred cash.** "I owe you $X, payable at first funding or revenue milestone." Document it. Some mission-aligned freelancers will accept this.
- **Barter.** Platform promotion, testimonial opportunities, or access to the SocialUs community as a selling point for their own business.

## Part 4: Recruitment Channels by Role Type

### Community / Organizer Roles
- **In person.** Farmers markets, co-op meetings, neighborhood association meetings, local food events, small business mixers. This is the only channel that matters for the first organizer.
- **Local Facebook groups.** Sacramento-area buy/sell/trade groups, neighborhood groups, food/farming groups.
- **University programs.** UC Davis Community Development, Sac State Public Policy, community food systems programs.
- **Nonprofit networks.** Sacramento Region Community Foundation, Valley Vision, Sacramento Food Policy Council.

### Advisor Roles (Legal, GTM, T&S)
- **Warm intros.** Ask every founder you know for one introduction. This is the highest-conversion channel.
- **GrowthMentor / ADPList / MentorCruise.** Platforms where advisors self-select for early-stage mentoring.
- **FAST agreement directory.** The Founder Institute's network.
- **Industry communities.** TSPA for T&S, local bar associations for legal, Reforge/Lenny's for growth.
- **LinkedIn.** Direct outreach with a clear, concise pitch. Lead with mission, not equity.

### Design Roles
- **Dribbble / Behance.** Search for community platform, marketplace, or civic tech work.
- **Purpose Jobs.** Mission-driven professionals specifically.
- **AIGA Sacramento.** Local design community.
- **Design Twitter/Threads.** Post the project, share the vision, attract interest.
- **ADPList.** Designers offering mentorship who might be interested in a deeper engagement.

### Future Full-Time Hires
- **Wellfound (AngelList).** Standard for startup hiring.
- **Purpose Jobs.** Mission-driven talent.
- **Hacker News "Who's Hiring."** When hiring engineers.
- **Local networks.** Sacramento is a small enough city that word-of-mouth scales.
- **The platform itself.** Once SocialUs has users, recruit from the community — people who already believe in the mission.

## Part 5: The "Join Us" Page — Concept

One visible **"Join Us"** button on the main site navigation. It leads to a single page with three clear paths. The page should feel warm, local, and direct — not corporate. No stock photos. Real places, real people (or honest illustrations until there are real photos).

### Page Structure

**Hero Section**
- Headline: **"Your community is building something."**
- Subline: "SocialUs is a platform for the people who grow your food, teach your kids, fix your pipes, and organize your neighborhood. We're just getting started — and there's a place for you."
- Three cards below, each a path.

**Path 1: Become a Member**
- *For:* Anyone who lives in or cares about a place.
- Headline: **"Find what's around you."**
- Copy: "Discover local producers, join events, follow what you love. Free, always."
- CTA: **"Sign Up"** → standard registration flow.

**Path 2: Bring What You Do**
- *For:* Producers, sellers, organizers, teachers, makers — anyone who has something to offer their community.
- Headline: **"Sell. Teach. Organize. Grow."**
- Copy: "Whether you're a baker with a Saturday stand, a fitness instructor starting a run club, or a neighbor with a skill to share — SocialUs gives you a place to be found and a community to serve. No middleman fees. No algorithm tax."
- Subsections (expandable or tabbed):
  - **Sell your products** — for farmers, bakers, craftspeople, makers
  - **Offer a service** — for trades, teachers, care providers
  - **Host an event** — for run clubs, book clubs, community events
  - **Start a group** — for any shared interest or practice
- CTA: **"Get Started"** → producer/organizer onboarding flow.

**Path 3: Build With Us**
- *For:* People who want to help build the platform itself.
- Headline: **"We're one founder and a lot of AI. We need you."**
- Copy: "SocialUs is built by one person with AI agents handling most of the code. What we can't automate is taste, trust, relationships, and judgment. We're looking for people who care about local communities and want to help build the infrastructure for collective action — not for a paycheck (yet), but because it matters."
- What we're looking for (not job listings — descriptions of the kind of help needed):
  - **Community champions** — people embedded in a local community who want to be the first to bring SocialUs to their town. "You know the vendors. You go to the meetings. You care about what happens on your block."
  - **Design** — "Make this thing beautiful. Help us build a visual identity that earns trust on first contact."
  - **Legal and policy** — "Help us get the rules right — content policy, privacy, marketplace compliance."
  - **Growth and marketing** — "Help us figure out how to tell this story so the right people hear it."
  - **Trust and safety** — "Help us build the moderation and dispute resolution framework before we need it."
- What we offer:
  - Equity in something being built for the right reasons
  - Founding team status — your name on the wall, permanently
  - Direct impact — your decisions shape the product
  - A front-row seat to building a platform with AI agents doing the heavy lifting
- CTA: **"Tell Us About Yourself"** → simple form: name, email, what you'd want to help with, a sentence about why this resonates. Not a job application.

### Design Notes
- **No job listings.** This isn't a careers page. It's an invitation page. The language should feel like a neighbor asking for help, not an HR department posting requisitions.
- **Local imagery.** Sacramento landmarks, farmers market photos, real neighborhoods. If no photos exist yet, use warm illustrations that evoke place and community — not abstract tech graphics.
- **One page, three paths.** Don't split these into separate pages. The power is in showing that "join us" means different things depending on who you are — and all of them matter equally.
- **Mobile-first.** Most people will hit this from a phone at a farmers market or after a friend shares a link.

### Copy Tone
- Direct. Warm. Slightly informal.
- Pro-competition, pro-local-economy framing: "No middleman fees. No algorithm tax. Your customers, your data, your community."
- American values: self-reliance, entrepreneurship, neighbor helping neighbor, small business as backbone.
- Honest about the stage: "We're early. We're small. That's why your help matters more now than it ever will again."

---

## Part 6: Immediate Next Steps

1. **Write the "Join Us" page.** Even before recruiting, this page establishes the identity and creates an inbound channel. Ship it as a static page on the MVP site.
2. **Identify the first Community Lead.** The founder should make a list of 5–10 people in the Sacramento local food / small business scene and have coffee with each one. The first organizer is the most important recruit.
3. **Find a designer.** Post the project on Dribbble or reach out to 3–5 designers whose portfolio shows community/marketplace work. Offer the hybrid cash/equity model.
4. **Set up FAST agreements.** Download FAST v3 from fi.co. Customize for SocialUs. Have it ready before any advisor conversation.
5. **Join advisor-matching platforms.** Create profiles on GrowthMentor and ADPList. Be specific about what you're building and what help you need.
6. **Attend local events as the founder.** Sacramento Startup Grind, local food policy meetings, farmers market association meetings. The founder IS the recruiting channel until there's a community lead.
