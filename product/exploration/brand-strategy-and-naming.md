# Brand Strategy & Naming Brief

> **Status:** Exploration. Strategic input for naming the platform, choosing a domain, and shaping the brand voice and visual identity. Does not produce the name — defines the target the name has to hit, the territory it can be drawn from, and the spine of the story the brand is part of.


## 1. What the platform is, in one breath

The platform is the substrate that lets a place act like a place again. It is the digital infrastructure for the porch, the market, the bulletin board, and the general store that extraction hollowed out — rebuilt with modern rails by people who now understand exactly what was taken and how.

It is not a local Amazon. Amazon is the thing that replaced the older shape; the platform is the older shape rebuilt. The older shape was not a worse version of e-commerce. It was a different category of thing entirely — a category where the transaction was the smallest part of what was happening, and the relationships, the showing-up, and the keeping-the-dollars-close were the point.


## 2. The narrative spine — return changed

The platform is part of a hero's-journey arc, played at the scale of a generation. People left the places they grew up in — sometimes literally, sometimes by withdrawing from them — because those places got hollowed out, extracted, made unaffordable, made unfriendly to the kind of life worth living. They learned, in their time away, exactly how the extraction works and exactly what was lost in the process. They are now coming back: not to a mythic past, but to the work of building places worth coming back to. They are bringing the changed perspective with them, and the rails to act on it.

This is not nostalgia. Nostalgia asks people to want a past they may never have had, and it codes politically in ways the platform's brief explicitly avoids. Return-changed is the opposite. It says: we lived inside the system, we saw how it works, and we are back with the tools to build something it cannot hollow out the same way. The Delaware PBC, the mission-lock vehicle, the profit cap, the federation protocol, the people-first object model — those are the elixir. They are not corporate-structure decisions; they are the changed perspective the hero brings home.

Every verb in the existing brief is a return verb. Take back. Reclaim. The muscle comes back with reps. The places people want to live still get built. The platform has been writing this story without naming it. This brief names it.


## 3. The offer — recovery of a muscle

The platform does not offer convenience. It offers the recovery of a capacity. The can-do spirit is not a memory; it is a muscle that atrophied from disuse, and it comes back with reps. The platform is the gym.

The first rep is small on purpose: host a gathering, list something you make, follow a venue, show up Saturday. The multiplier math is real: one purchase from a local maker is three cents of a city's GDP; ten percent of a zip code doing it monthly puts real payroll back in real hands. The work is collective by definition — none of it happens alone — and the platform's job is to make the assembling and the joining-in easy.


## 4. The voice — serious without self-serious

The brand voice is cheeky defiance. Patagonia-earnest-funny, not co-op-earnest-only. The people behind this know exactly how dire the extraction story is and are choosing to be funny about it anyway, because despair is the system's preferred state for the people it extracts from. Humor is not a softener bolted on top of the message. It is part of the message — it signals that the project is run by people who have not been broken by what they are working against.

The defining tonal artifact is the penguin holding a knife under the phrase "flight was never an option." That image is the brand voice in compressed form. It is funny. It is structurally serious. It is defiant without being aggressive. It does not name a villain. It does not ask for permission. It announces that the people in the huddle are staying — and that they are not soft.

The voice can hold both the porch conversation and the rallying cry. The naming work should preserve that range.


## 5. The mascot — why the penguin earns this role

The penguin is not a decorative choice. It is structurally aligned with the platform's mission in a way few mascots are.

Penguins cannot fly. They cannot flee. They have to make it work where they are. That is the anti-extraction posture made literal.

Penguins survive cold storms by huddling. Mutual aid is not a metaphor for them; it is the mechanism. The huddle rotates — birds on the outside trade places with birds on the inside — so the cost of warmth is shared. That is cooperative shape made literal.

Emperor penguins return to the same rookery every breeding season and find their specific mate by voice in a colony of thousands. Fidelity to place is not a thematic add-on; it is their defining behavior. The huddle, the colony, the rookery, and the return are the same story. The mascot is, structurally, a return-to-place creature.

The penguin will appear in many poses and with many objects, leading the charge in different ways. The knife is one register; the basket, the hammer, the ballot, the seedling, the meeting agenda, the coffee cup are others. The colony is the platform. Each member is a penguin. The cold storm is extraction. The brand can draw on this ecosystem indefinitely.


## 6. The brand architecture — three layers

The brand operates on three layers, each doing the work it is best at.

**Layer One — The Studio.** The maker behind the platform. The Don-Claude-Sam (or Don-Claude-Pam) credit line, echoing the Jean-Claude Van Damme cadence as an inside joke for the people who spot it. Sits on the about page and the credits, not on the front door. Plays the role A24, Pixar, or Studio Ghibli play for the products they make.

**Layer Two — The Platform.** The consumer-facing brand. The penguin lives here. "Flight was never an option" is the rallying line — reserved for the manifesto page and the merchandise, not the homepage headline. This layer needs the name.

**Layer Three — The Feature.** Pop Shop is the name for the local-business listing surface inside the platform. It does the mom-and-pop / pop-up double-duty without having to carry the whole platform's framing.

The naming work is for Layer Two.


## 7. Naming territories — where the name can be drawn from

Five thematic territories pull on the platform's mission. The strongest candidate names will likely braid two of them — a name drawn from only one tends to land either too cute or too generic. The territories, ordered roughly by strength of pull:

**Penguin-collective words.** The actual nouns for groups of penguins are unusually generative: colony, huddle, waddle, rookery, raft. Because the mascot is already a penguin, anything from this set arrives pre-loaded with meaning. Huddle carries mutual aid. Waddle carries forward motion together with a built-in wink. Rookery is sturdier and older-feeling. Colony names the platform-as-organism.

**Place and commons words.** Commons, square, porch, block, corner, hearth, plaza, market, ground. Names the where, which is the platform's organizing principle — locality is the unit. These words resist the Pop Shop problem of narrowing to commerce; a commons hosts a market, a meeting, a cleanup, a conversation. Porch in particular pairs well with the tone guidance in the original brief ("porch conversation, not manifesto").

**Return and root words.** Home, return, back, root, ground, again, reweave, rebuild, plant. This territory was unlocked by naming the return-changed spine. Encodes the hero's-journey arc directly. Works as a verb-shaped name (something you do here) more than as a noun. Pairs especially well with place words and with collective words.

**Standing-ground words.** Stay, hold, anchor, plant, ground. Encodes "flight was never an option" structurally rather than as a tagline. Quiet, sturdy, defiant without being combative. Reads as a posture more than as a place.

**First-action words.** Rep, step, raise, show, hand. Matches the "first rep" frame — make the first action easy. Kinetic and inviting. Risk alone: can feel fitness-app-ish; strongest when paired with a place or collective word.

The strongest braids are likely return + place, collective + place, or return + collective.


## 8. What to avoid

Villain-naming. Blame-laying. Partisan signaling. Doom framing. Scarcity framing. Scolding or lecturing register. Names that read as activist before they read as warm. Names that pre-narrow the platform to commerce (the Pop Shop problem at the platform level). Names that require an insider story to make sense (the Don-Claude-Sam joke at the platform level). Names that ask the visitor to already understand the mission before they can read the name.

Words to keep off the list at the platform-name level: resist, fight, revolt, uprising, war, battle, crisis, save, rescue. These all break the cheeky-defiant register and pull the brand toward the manifesto tone the original brief explicitly avoids.


## 9. Shape of a strong name

The name the founder chooses will need to work as a URL, an app name, a brand mark, and a word people say out loud when telling a friend. These are the structural criteria it should meet.

**Length and phonetics.** One or two syllables is ideal; three is the ceiling. It should be easy to say, easy to spell from hearing it, and easy to type on a phone keyboard. No silent letters, no ambiguous spelling (is it -er or -or? -le or -el?). The word should land cleanly when spoken aloud — no mush, no swallowed sounds.

**Domain shape.** The .com is the goal. If .com is taken, the name should be short enough that a two-word .com works (word + collective noun, word + place noun). Alternatives worth considering: .co, .club, .community, or a clean exact-match .io as a placeholder until .com is acquirable. Avoid hyphens, numbers, or "get/try/join" prefixes — those signal a startup that couldn't get the real domain. The URL should be the name, nothing added.

**App store readability.** The name will sit in a 30-character app-name field alongside an icon (the penguin). It needs to be scannable at small sizes. Names that are too long or too clever lose in the app store scroll. The name should make sense standing alone, without the tagline.

**Verb potential.** The strongest platform names become verbs ("google it," "venmo me"). A name with natural verb shape — something people could say as an action ("let's ___ it," "I'll ___ that") — has a structural advantage. This is not required, but it's a multiplier.

**Warmth before mission.** A stranger encountering the name cold — no context, no manifesto — should feel warmth, curiosity, or invitation before they feel mission. The mission earns its weight after the first visit. The name earns the click. If the name reads as a cause before it reads as a place, it will filter out the people the platform most needs to reach.

**Tone range.** The name needs to hold the porch register (casual, neighborly, Saturday-morning) and the rally register (defiant, collective, serious-underneath) without breaking in either direction. Test: can you put the name on a tote bag and on a legal filing and have both feel right?

**Scalability.** The name should still fit when the platform is facilitating shared banking, equipment co-ops, mutual aid, and cross-community federation — not just Saturday farmers markets. It should not encode a specific activity (markets), a specific geography (local-), or a specific relationship (neighbor-) so tightly that growth requires explaining why the name no longer means what it says.

**The braid test.** The strongest names from Section 7 will braid two territories. A good self-test: can you trace the name back to two of the five territory families? If it only maps to one, it's probably too narrow. If it maps to three or more, it's probably too abstract.

**Mascot harmony.** The penguin will sit next to this name on every surface. The name should not compete with the mascot for attention or for metaphor space. If the name is already an animal word, the penguin becomes redundant. If the name is too abstract, the penguin has to do all the emotional work alone. The sweet spot: the name and the penguin reinforce each other without being the same idea.
