# Project arc — for design briefing

A simple, high-level read of what this platform is, the story it lives inside, and the mascot candidates considered along the way. Written to stay true through small feature changes, so it doesn't need constant updating.

## What it is, in one breath

The substrate that lets a place act like a place again. The digital version of the porch, the market, the bulletin board, and the general store that extraction hollowed out — rebuilt with modern rails by people who now understand exactly what was taken and how.

Not a local Amazon. Amazon is the thing that replaced the older shape; the platform is the older shape rebuilt. In that older shape, the transaction was the smallest part of what was happening — relationships, showing up, and keeping dollars close were the point.

## The arc — return changed

This is a hero's-journey arc played at the scale of a generation. People left the places they grew up in — sometimes physically, sometimes by withdrawal — because those places got hollowed out, extracted, made unaffordable, made unfriendly. They learned, in their time away, exactly how the extraction works and what was lost in the process. They are now coming back: not to a mythic past, but to the work of building places worth coming back to. They bring the changed perspective with them, and the rails to act on it.

This is not nostalgia. Nostalgia asks people to want a past they may never have had, and it codes politically in ways the platform avoids. Return-changed says the opposite: we lived inside the system, we saw how it works, and we are back with the tools to build something it cannot hollow out the same way.

Every verb in the platform's language is a return verb. Take back. Reclaim. The muscle comes back with reps. The places people want to live still get built.

## What the platform offers

Not convenience — the recovery of a capacity. The can-do spirit is a muscle that atrophied from disuse, and it comes back with reps. The platform is the gym. The first rep is small on purpose: host a gathering, list something you make, follow a venue, show up Saturday. The multiplier math is real: one purchase from a local maker is three cents of a city's GDP; ten percent of a zip code doing it monthly puts real payroll back in real hands.

## The voice — cheeky defiance

Patagonia-earnest-funny, not co-op-earnest-only. The people behind this know exactly how dire the extraction story is and are choosing to be funny about it anyway, because despair is the system's preferred state for the people it extracts from. Humor is part of the message, not a softener bolted on top — it signals that the project is run by people who have not been broken by what they are working against.

The voice holds two registers at once: the porch (casual, neighborly, Saturday-morning) and the rally (defiant, collective, serious-underneath). Look and feel should hold both without breaking in either direction. Test: would the same thing feel right on a tote bag and on a legal filing?

## Mascot candidates — and what each was considered for

Three options have been on the table. Each maps to a different job the mascot might do.

### The Spicy Penguin — day-to-day brand

A classic upright penguin silhouette, round and friendly, holding whatever the moment calls for: basket at the market, hammer at a build day, sign at a council meeting, fork at a potluck. Same character, dozens of situations.

The structural fit. Penguins cannot fly. They cannot flee. They have to make it work where they are — the anti-extraction posture made literal. They survive cold storms by huddling, with the huddle rotating so the cost of warmth is shared — cooperative shape made literal. Emperor penguins return to the same rookery every breeding season and find their specific mate by voice in a colony of thousands — fidelity to place as their defining behavior.

What it wins on. Universal warmth. A prop system that scales the brand across contexts without changing the character. Underdog charm that survives every variant. Easy first impression — grandparents and kids both find it approachable.

What it gives up. Penguins endure; they don't organize or push back. Antarctica isn't the American backyard. A single bird can't carry the collective imagery of many neighbors coordinating.

### The Knife Penguin — reserved inside-baseball rally

A rare variant of the Spicy Penguin: the bird with a knife under "flight was never an option." Reserved for "we're preparing for a political fight" moments — advocacy pushes, anti-extraction stories, election-adjacent organizing.

Why it earns a separate slot. The image is the cheeky-defiance voice in compressed form. Funny. Structurally serious. Defiant without being aggressive. It does not name a villain. It does not ask for permission. It announces that the people in the huddle are staying — and that they are not soft. Used sparingly, it keeps its charge. Used as the default, it leaks edgy and breaks the warmth.

### The Crow Flock — collective metaphor

A flock of stylized crows. The character comes from being many, not from props: birds gathered on a wire, lifting together off a roof, posted as sentinels, settling in for a dusk roost.

The structural fit. Crows recognize individual neighbors, remember favors, share food locations, mob threats together, and hold territory across generations. Endorsement memory, information substrate, collective pushback on extraction, place-attached families — every platform feature has a crow behavior behind it. And crows are American everywhere — every backyard, parking lot, county fair, small-town main street. Already on the porch, no zoo required.

What it wins on. The flock IS the platform — neighbors who notice, remember, share, and collectively show up. Active rather than enduring; mobbing reads as "take back / reclaim" without needing words.

What it gives up. Lower cold-start warmth — some people find crows unsettling, more so as a flock. Cultural baggage: "murder of crows," Poe, death omens. Palette skews dark, which tilts the whole visual identity heavier. One image, not a system — variants have to come from styling rather than swappable props.

### How the three could co-exist

Spicy Penguin as the day-to-day external face. Knife Penguin reserved for rare political-fight moments so it keeps its charge. Crow flock as a conceptual frame in copy — "neighbors who notice, remember, and show up for each other" — without having to carry the visual brand. Or: commit fully to one bird and don't split the brand. Both paths are open.

## What this means for look and feel

The look and feel earns the click before it earns the cause. Warmth, curiosity, and invitation read first; the mission reveals itself on the second look. A stranger arriving cold should feel like they were already invited.

It holds two registers. The porch (Saturday morning, neighborly, casual). The rally (defiant, collective, sturdy-underneath). The same surfaces should be at home in both moods — nothing so cute it breaks under weight, nothing so serious it scolds.

Place is the organizing unit. The visual identity should feel rooted somewhere specific — not a global tech aesthetic. American without jingoism. Small-town without parody. Modern without sterile.

People first, not businesses first. The visual language centers the human doing the thing, not the corporate shell behind it. A face, a name, a hand on a basket — these read true. A logo wall reads wrong.

Underdog energy is the throughline. Small actors doing real things in real places. The mascot — whichever it ends up being — earns its role by being structurally aligned with that posture, not by being decorative.
