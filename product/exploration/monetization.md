# Monetization Exploration

Post-beta revenue paths, organized by timeline.

---

## Near-term (Launch → 12 months)

**Freemium Business Listings** — Verified, enhanced business profiles for a monthly fee ($15–50/mo). Basic listing is free; paid tiers unlock analytics, promotional features, and a "verified independent" badge.

**Transaction Fees on Direct Sales** — For the farmer/rancher and maker marketplace, take 3–8% per transaction. Scales naturally with GMV and aligns incentives with seller success.

**Consumer Subscription** — $3–6/month for power users: offline maps, advanced filters, exclusive deals from local businesses. Keep the core app free to drive adoption.

## Medium-term (Year 1–2)

**"Shop Local" Gift Cards / Vouchers** — Sell multi-business gift cards where participating businesses collectively honor them. Small float or redemption fee. Drives consumer engagement and merchant lock-in.

**Cooperative Membership Model** — Businesses pay a higher annual fee (~$200–500/yr) in exchange for co-op governance benefits: voting rights on features, etc. Fits the pro-competition ethos and creates community ownership.

**CSA / Pre-order Facilitation** — Provide infrastructure for subscription boxes and take a platform fee. Highly sticky once farmers are onboarded.

## Medium-term — Standalone Revenue Stream

**Business Intelligence Platform** — World-class analysis tools for independent businesses, priced separately from listings. Democratizes the data capabilities that big chains and PE-owned competitors rely on. Three tiers:

*T1 — Demand Intelligence:* Local search trends, category demand gaps ("nobody near you sells X"), seasonal patterns, consumer interest heatmaps. The "what do people want" layer.

*T2 — Competitive Benchmarking:* Anonymized performance comparisons — how does your listing/store engage vs. similar businesses in your area? Percentile rankings, not competitor naming. "Your profile engagement is in the top 20% of bakeries within 25 miles."

*T3 — Supplier Recommendations & Action Tools:* The advisory layer. "Demand for locally roasted coffee is rising in your area — here are three regional roasters." Connects supply and demand across the network. Potential evolution into a B2B marketplace where independent businesses source from independent suppliers.

**Privacy guardrail:** Businesses see aggregated trends, never individual user data. This is non-negotiable and a differentiator from big-tech analytics platforms.

**Dependency:** Requires meaningful consumer traffic to generate valuable signals. Design the data pipeline early; monetize once volume justifies it.

**Note:** The same data pipeline feeds the longer-term "Data & Insights" line below (anonymized trend data sold to governments/researchers). Different packaging, different buyer, same infrastructure.

## Longer-term

**White-label / Licensing** — Local chambers of commerce, credit unions, and BIDs (Business Improvement Districts) pay for a branded version. Natural institutional partners with existing budgets.

**Data & Insights (Anonymized)** — Aggregate consumer trend data sold to local governments, economic development orgs, or academic researchers — not advertisers. Keeps values intact.

## Prioritization

Given the need to generate revenue quickly:

1. **Transaction fees** from the marketplace (zero upfront cost to merchants, self-funding)
2. **Verified listing fees** once there's enough consumer traffic to make listings valuable
3. **Consumer subscription** only after building a loyal user base

The cooperative membership model is worth designing into the governance structure early even if it's not the first revenue driver — it's a major differentiator for storytelling and fundraising with aligned sources like CDFIs, community foundations, or revenue-based financing.
