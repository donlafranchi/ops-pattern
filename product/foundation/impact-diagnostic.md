# Impact Diagnostic — Benefits the Few vs the Many

> Companion to [`principles.md`](principles.md). The constitution names *wealth circulation over wealth extraction* as the single thing the platform holds to. This doc is the diagnostic for assessing impact: how to recognize when an organization's benefits flow to the few at the expense of the many, and the toolkit of Member-aligned treatments the platform can scaffold in response. The point isn't to be against any industry — it's to see clearly who a given arrangement serves, and to scaffold alternatives where the answer is "the few."

---

## The Diagnostic — Five Markers

Apply to any service, industry, or vendor. The more markers it hits, the higher the leverage from intervention.

1. **Profit grows when customer cost grows.** Vendor profit is structured as a percentage of total spend, so the vendor wins more when total spend rises. The Medical Loss Ratio pattern. Found in: insurance (all flavors), financial services, healthcare delivery, professional services billed by the hour, attention-economy media.

2. **Repair and durability suppressed.** Planned obsolescence, parts monopolization, software-locked hardware, service-only-by-authorized-dealer. Found in: cars, appliances, electronics, agricultural equipment, medical devices, increasingly software-locked goods of every kind.

3. **Switching costs artificial.** Proprietary parts, opaque contracts, regulatory moats, network lock-in, identity portability blocked. Found in: telecom, software-as-a-service, banking, healthcare provider networks, gig-economy platforms.

4. **Information asymmetry captured.** Vendor knows the true cost; customer cannot see it. Found in: healthcare pricing, auto repair, funeral services, legal billing, real estate commission, college tuition discounting.

5. **Regulatory environment captured.** Vendor has lobbied the rules that protect their position. Found in: insurance carve-outs, occupational licensing, certificate-of-need, single-family zoning, agricultural marketing orders, dealer-franchise laws.

A service hitting one marker may still be aligned with Members; a service hitting three or more is extracting. The platform's leverage scales with the number of markers — health insurance hits all five (highest extraction, highest leverage); auto insurance hits one, three, and four (lighter regulation, more achievable target); housing hits one, three, and five.

## The Treatment Toolkit — Five Patterns

The platform scaffolds Member-aligned alternatives. Each industry uses some subset of these; none requires all five.

1. **Realign profit via ownership.** Member-owned cooperative form returns surplus to Members rather than external shareholders. Works wherever ownership structure is mutable — most consumer services, most labor-heavy services, most local infrastructure.

2. **Restore information symmetry.** Transparent pricing, peer-shared experience, vendor scorecards, repair-difficulty ratings, durability comparisons. Cheap to scaffold; cuts the single largest source of extraction.

3. **Reduce switching costs.** Portable Member identity, public APIs, interoperability requirements written into platform partnerships, anti-lock-in by design. The platform's own commitment to this becomes a competitive moat — incumbents cannot match it without giving up their own extraction.

4. **Build countervailing power.** Federation, group negotiation, collective Member purchasing, joint legal action. Works wherever scale matters more than capital.

5. **Advocate for policy reform.** Right-to-repair laws, occupational licensing reform, antitrust enforcement, single-family zoning reform, mandatory price transparency, MLR-style profit-cap rules extended to other industries. Members lobby through the platform; the platform itself stays infrastructure but provides the substrate. This is the lever that changes the rules under which extraction operates.

## Applied Across Industries

The same pattern with different surfaces.

- **Health insurance.** MLR perverse incentive (marker 1) → member-owned mutual or chartered nonprofit partnership + price transparency + policy advocacy.
- **Auto insurance.** Same pattern, lighter regulation (markers 1, 3, 4) → member-owned mutual. State Farm, USAA, and Liberty Mutual all started this way. Roughly 50 mutual auto insurers still operate.
- **Auto manufacturing and repair.** Planned obsolescence + parts monopoly (markers 2, 3, 5) → right-to-repair advocacy + Member-rated independent mechanics + cooperative repair shops + collective parts purchasing.
- **Housing.** Rent extraction + zoning capture (markers 1, 3, 5) → Community Land Trust + Limited Equity Housing Cooperative + zoning reform advocacy.
- **Food and agriculture.** Processor monopsony + ag-marketing orders (markers 1, 5) → farmer cooperatives, consumer-grocery cooperatives, Capper-Volstead antitrust treatment.
- **Broadband.** Utility monopoly + regulatory capture (markers 3, 5) → municipal fiber + cooperative broadband (~900 operating cooperatives nationally, mostly successors to rural electric).
- **Banking.** Predatory products + fee extraction + opacity (markers 1, 4) → credit union partnership + Member-owned community banking.
- **Childcare.** Consolidation + worker squeeze + opacity (markers 1, 4) → parent cooperative + worker-owned childcare cooperative.
- **Professional services.** Hourly billing + opacity + captured licensing (markers 1, 4, 5) → flat-fee Member-rated alternatives + group purchasing + licensing-reform advocacy.

## How the Platform Engages

The platform is infrastructure, not parent company. It scaffolds the formation of Member-aligned alternatives; it does not become one. Three stacked layers — *pool* (conditional commitment mechanics), *form* (templated cooperative incorporation per state and sector), *federate* (shared back-office, joint negotiation) — let Vienna-scale outcomes emerge from thousands of small Member-owned entities federated together, rather than from a single subsidiary holding company.

See [`principles.md`](principles.md) for the constitutional framing. The single commitment (wealth circulation over wealth extraction) operates as the destination test; this doc operationalizes how to assess whether an arrangement benefits the few or the many, and how to respond. All treatments are expressed as defaults — laws change, technology changes, conditions change. Patterns expressed as defaults adapt; categorical refusals lock the platform to today's stance.
