# Promises

These three, and nothing else. Earlier public-voice commitments were written into the docs without the PM's consent and are withdrawn — a promise not on this page isn't a promise, however confidently an older document states it. **Nothing is published** — the app hasn't launched, every promise-shaped string in the repo is a draft, and what gets published is the PM's call alone.

## 1. Revenue is ordinary. Surplus goes back to the community. *(internal-only — see § Open question)*

We make money in normal, well-understood ways; what's left over after running it goes back into the community it came from. Rules out: revenue that only works if a member is stuck, and any surplus quietly becoming someone's return.

## 2. Every decision is weighed on member benefit against product benefit

Nothing ships that only helps the product — if it doesn't also help the people using it, it doesn't happen. Rules out: anything justified purely by retention or growth, and any trade where the member's cost is real and their benefit theoretical. This is the one with teeth day to day — every decision record and scenario should be able to fill in "who benefits, member and product, and what does the member give up." Not yet enforced anywhere mechanically.

## 3. We are not an extractive platform

Not Amazon, not Airbnb, not another app moving wealth out of a place. Rules out: a cut that grows with a member's dependence, selling member data, owning the relationship between two people who found each other here.

## Guidelines — a tier below promises

Guidelines are strong defaults, departed from only with a recorded reason that clears promise 2 — not a weaker promise, a different kind of thing. Departure procedure: name the mechanic, show who benefits and what the member gives up, record it with a date in `DECISIONS.md`. An undocumented departure is a violation, not a judgment call.

- **Visibility isn't sold by default.** Departing means a specific paid-placement mechanic that clears the member-benefit gate on its own — "it funds the platform" is the product half stated twice and doesn't clear it.
- **Fees follow success; they never gate entry.** People participate meaningfully without paying, and pay meaningfully only once they've succeeded. Hard to imagine a defensible departure — kept as a guideline rather than a promise anyway, because a guideline forces the reasoning into the open where an absolute would hide behind technical compliance.

## Promise candidates — drafted, not ratified

Two candidates exist, deliberately unratified, and must not be cited as promises: **(A)** members share in what they help build, silent on amount/mechanism by design — the moment a number appears it becomes a financial instrument rather than a commitment. **(B)** the platform's responsibility runs to members and communities, not outside capital, and takes no outside shareholders (firm intent, not a plan — but "outside" is load-bearing: a bare "no shareholders" would also rule out employee equity and member-held co-op shares, which a union-shaped model wants available).

**The steer underneath both: closer to a union than a company** — funds itself through dues and membership, earns its keep by delivering services and bargaining power to the people who belong to it. This may resolve candidate A's ambiguity too: if "share" means union-style services and a say rather than a cut of profit, it doesn't compete with promise 1 for the same dollar at all. See `DECISIONS.md` for the open A/B/C on promise 1's actual definition.

## How good faith is enforced

**By community and peer pressure, not platform policing.** This is not an anonymous internet platform; it's a know-and-support-your-community platform — a vendor who claims to be somewhere they aren't is seen by their own followers, and the social cost lands immediately without the platform doing anything. This is why appearances are auto-approved rather than gatekept: an approval queue treats a false claim as something the platform must prevent, and in a place where the claimant's neighbours can see the claim, prevention is work the community does better and faster. If approval ever becomes cheap to build, that alone isn't a reason to add it back.

**Open tension, not to be resolved by whoever ships the first feature that touches it:** letting people flag bad-faith behavior is one step from a reputation system, and reviews/ratings/reputation scores are permanently refused under promise 3 and the no-ranking-a-person commitment. Community accountability needs the community to be able to say something; the refusal exists because a rating economy makes the platform the arbiter of who gets business. How accountability works without becoming the rating economy is unanswered. Not a launch problem — nothing at launch lets a member say anything public about another member — but it will arrive, and the first proposal that looks like a solution will look like a rating.
