# Promises

> **Ratified 2026-09-07 by the PM directly.** Not through `weigh` — recorded here so the shortcut stays visible.
>
> **These three, and nothing else.** Earlier public-voice commitments were written into the docs without the PM's consent and are **withdrawn** — see § Withdrawn. A promise that isn't on this page isn't a promise, however confidently an older document states it. This page supersedes anything that conflicts.
>
> **Promise 1 is internal-only until the definitional question below is answered.** Do not publish it.

---

## 1. Revenue is ordinary. Surplus goes back to the community. *(internal-only — see § The open question)*

We make money in normal, well-understood ways, and that money pays for the product. What's left over after running it goes back into the community it came from.

**Why:** the shape of the revenue is what determines whether a platform ends up serving the place or draining it.

**Rules out:** revenue that only works if a member is stuck, and any surplus quietly becoming someone's return.

## 2. Every decision is weighed on member benefit against product benefit

Nothing ships that only helps the product. If it doesn't also help the people using it, it doesn't happen.

**Why:** this is the one that has teeth day to day — it's a gate on every feature, not a sentiment.

**Rules out:** anything justified purely by retention, conversion, or platform growth; and any trade where the member's cost is real and their benefit is theoretical.

## 3. We are not an extractive platform

Not Amazon. Not Airbnb. Not another large app moving wealth out of a place. We're the opposite of that.

**Why:** it's the umbrella — the other two are how this one gets kept.

**Rules out:** taking a cut that grows with a member's dependence, selling member data, and owning the relationship between two people who found each other here.

---

## The open question — promise 1 is not publishable yet

**"Above what it costs to operate" has no defensible meaning yet, and a money promise gets held to the letter.** Three things are undefined: who decides the number, over what period, and what "back into the community" concretely means. Options, with the trade-off:

- **A — Named percentage, annual, into a community fund.** Cleanest to say and to audit; commits to a specific number before there's any revenue to test it against.
- **B — Surplus defined as revenue minus operating cost and a stated founder salary, published annually.** Honest and defensible, and it makes the salary public — which is either the most credible thing on the page or the most uncomfortable.
- **C — Direction without a number: "we're not structured to extract, and we publish where the money goes."** Safe to publish today and impossible to break; also the weakest, and close enough to promise 3 that it may not earn its own line.

**Recommendation: C to launch, B as the target once there is revenue to report.** A commits to arithmetic nobody can do yet.

**Until the PM picks, promise 1 stays out of user-facing copy** and promises 2 and 3 carry the public voice alone.

---

## Nothing is published

**The app is not launched.** Strings in the codebase are drafts. No promise-shaped line anywhere in this repo has been made to anyone, and none of it should be described as live, published, or already read.

**What gets published, and when, is the PM's call and nobody else's.** Until he says so, the working assumption is that nothing is.

**The practical consequence:** a promise-shaped line in draft copy is removed, not retracted. There is nobody to retract it to.

---

## Removed

Each of these was written as a commitment. None was agreed to. All are gone from the drafts.

- **"You will never pay for visibility."** **Advertising and paid placement are not banned by fiat** — any specific mechanic passes promise 2 before it ships, and promise 2 is a real gate.
- **"The structure of the company will reflect the structure of the promise — something members own."** No decision supports it.
- **"You will leave the platform stronger than you arrived."** Survives as a description of intent, nowhere as a commitment.
- **"Your relationships with your customers belong to you — never sold, never gated."** The never-sold half follows from promise 3. The never-gated half is a product commitment nobody made.
- **"Your growth is the metric."** The measurement commitment lives in the metrics doc, and it does not say this.
- **"Free forever," "no fees, ever," "no listing fees, no transaction fees."** Money promises across three draft surfaces, all from one microcopy recipe. Replaced with what is true in the present tense: *listing costs nothing.*

## The three previously-unratified items, re-tested against the promises

- **"Reviews and star ratings are permanently deferred."** **Keep** — promise 3. A rating economy is how a platform makes itself the arbiter of who gets business, which is the extraction pattern in its most familiar form.
- **"A values statement is never sourced or inferred."** **Keep** — promise 2. A label the platform attaches to a person it can place on a map is benefit-to-product, cost-to-member, which is exactly what promise 2 forbids.
- **"Nothing ships that cannot be taken down."** **Keep, but on operational grounds, not constitutional ones.** No promise requires it. It stays because serving an image you have no way to remove is an unbounded liability with no recovery path — that is an operations argument, and it should be recorded as one rather than dressed as a principle.

---

## Making promise 2 a gate rather than a sentiment

Promise 2 is the only one that can be checked on a Tuesday, and right now nothing checks it. **Every decision record and every scenario needs one line: *who benefits, member and product, and what does the member give up?*** A decision that cannot fill in the member half has failed the promise, and that is the finding — not a prompt to write something reassuring in the box.

Owed: a member-benefit line in the decision-log format, in the scenario template, and in `weigh`'s close-out. Not yet done.
