# Promises

> **Ratified 2026-09-07 by the PM directly.** Not through `weigh` — recorded here so the shortcut stays visible.
>
> **These three, and nothing else.** Earlier public-voice commitments were written into the docs without the PM's consent and are **withdrawn** — see § Withdrawn. A promise that isn't on this page isn't a promise, however confidently an older document states it. This page supersedes anything that conflicts.
>
> **Promise 1 is internal-only until the definitional question below is answered.** Do not publish it.

---

## 1. Revenue is ordinary. Surplus goes back to the community. *(internal-only — see § The open question)*

We make money in normal, well-understood ways, and that money pays for the product. What's left over after running it goes back into the community it came from.

**Why:** the shape of the revenue is what determines whether a platform ends up serving the place or draining it.

**Rules out:** revenue that only works if a member is stuck, and any surplus quietly becoming someone's return.

## 2. Every decision is weighed on member benefit against product benefit

Nothing ships that only helps the product. If it doesn't also help the people using it, it doesn't happen.

**Why:** this is the one that has teeth day to day — it's a gate on every feature, not a sentiment.

**Rules out:** anything justified purely by retention, conversion, or platform growth; and any trade where the member's cost is real and their benefit is theoretical.

## 3. We are not an extractive platform

Not Amazon. Not Airbnb. Not another large app moving wealth out of a place. We're the opposite of that.

**Why:** it's the umbrella — the other two are how this one gets kept.

**Rules out:** taking a cut that grows with a member's dependence, selling member data, and owning the relationship between two people who found each other here.

# Guidelines

**Added 2026-09-07.** A tier below promises. **Promises are absolute and few. Guidelines are strong defaults that can be departed from with a recorded reason** — and the reason has to clear the member-benefit gate in promise 2. A guideline is not a weaker promise; it is a different kind of thing. The test for which tier something belongs in: *would departing from this ever be defensible?* If yes, it is a guideline, and pretending otherwise just produces a promise the project quietly breaks.

**Departure procedure:** name the specific mechanic, show who benefits on the member side and what the member gives up, record it in the decision log with the date. **A departure with no recorded reason is a violation, not a judgment call.**

## Guideline 1 — we don't sell visibility

Ranking, placement and reach are not for sale. That is the default, not a ban.

**Departing from it** means a specific paid-placement or advertising mechanic that clears the member-benefit gate on its own merits. "It funds the platform" is the product half stated twice and does not clear it.

**Why it's a guideline and not a promise:** an absolute here was tried and withdrawn on 2026-09-07 because nobody had agreed to it. The default is right; the absolute was borrowed confidence.

## Guideline 2 — never price out the small or the unsuccessful

People can participate meaningfully without paying. **Fees follow success; they never gate entry.** We help small creators build something and they pay meaningfully only once they have found success.

*Merged 2026-09-07 from two separate monetization refusals — "charging the small operator before they succeed" and "subscriptions disguised as gates" — which were the same commitment said twice from two directions.*

**Departing from it** would mean charging someone before they have earned anything, and it is hard to imagine a defensible version. **Kept as a guideline rather than a promise anyway**, because the failure mode of an absolute here is a pricing model that technically complies and practically excludes — and a guideline forces the reasoning into the open where an absolute lets it hide behind compliance.

# Promise candidates — drafted 2026-09-07, NOT ratified

**These are drafts. They are not promises yet and must not be cited as any.** Written down so the wording can be argued with before it hardens.

## Candidate A — members share in what they help build

*Draft wording:* **"The people who help build this share in what it becomes. If you helped it grow, you benefit from it growing."**

**Deliberately silent on amount and mechanism.** The commitment is *that* participants share, not how much or in what form. That silence is the point and should survive drafting — the moment a number or a mechanism appears, this becomes a financial instrument rather than a commitment.

**The ambiguity that has to be resolved before ratification:** *share* could mean a cut of surplus, or it could mean ownership. Those are different promises with different consequences, and the wording currently covers both. See § The overlap below.

## Candidate B — our responsibility runs to members and communities

*Draft wording:* **"Our responsibility runs to the people who use this and the places they live. We are not taking outside shareholders — and if the structure ever changes, the order of responsibility does not."**

**Upgraded 2026-09-07 (same day, second ruling).** The no-outside-shareholders half was drafted as a plan; the PM corrected that to **firm intent** — he strongly does not want shareholders. The earlier hedge is superseded, and the correction is recorded rather than silently applied.

**The two halves, now both firm but doing different work:**

- **Whose interests come first** — members and communities ahead of outside capital. Constrains *priority*.
- **No outside shareholders** — firm intent, not a plan. Constrains *structure*.

### The steer underneath it: closer to a union than a company

The PM's framing is **a union, not a company** — and it is a design steer, not a metaphor. A union funds itself through dues and membership, earns its keep by delivering services and collective bargaining power to the people who belong to it, and answers to those people rather than to capital. **That is a coherent revenue model, not a slogan**, and it is the model the rest of the money thinking should be built against.

**It may also answer candidate A on its own.** A union member does not hold equity and does not receive a dividend; they get *bargaining power, services, and a say* — value that flows to participants without ever being a claim on profit. If "members share in what they help build" means that, then **it does not compete with the surplus promise at all**, and the profit-versus-ownership question dissolves rather than needing a priority order. See § The overlap.

### Consequence accepted, and the wording that has to protect against it

**No equity round.** The PM has accepted this. Revenue therefore has to come from everywhere else, and especially early — see [`monetization.md`](monetization.md) § Non-equity revenue paths.

**Flagged once, not argued:** "no shareholders" stated flatly also rules out **employee equity** and **member-held co-op shares**, because both are technically shares. **A union model wants the second of those available.** The draft wording above says *outside* shareholders for exactly this reason — it closes the door to capital without closing it to the people doing the work or the people using the platform. **If the wording is ever tightened to bare "no shareholders," that protection is lost.**

## The reversal, recorded — both dates

**2026-09-07, earlier today: member-ownership was withdrawn.** It appeared in the promise doc as *"the structure of the company will reflect the structure of the promise — something members own,"* no decision record supported it, and the PM had never consented to it. It was the agent's promise.

**2026-09-07, later the same day: the PM reinstated a version he wrote himself** — candidates A and B above.

**The distinction is the whole point and is not a formality.** The withdrawn line and the reinstated ones say overlapping things. What changed is not the content but the authority: the first was written into the repo on the project's behalf without being agreed; the second is the PM's own. **A reversal of that shape is legitimate, and recording it as though the withdrawal never happened would erase exactly the lesson that produced the promise audit.** Both dates stand.

## The overlap — promise 1 and candidate A are not the same, and they compete

**Asked: is "surplus goes back to the community" the same commitment as "members share in what they help build"?**

**No. They differ in recipient and in basis, and they draw on the same pot.**

- **Promise 1 is collective and place-based.** Surplus returns to *the community* — a fund, local investment, something a place holds. A member who contributed nothing benefits equally with one who contributed everything. That is a feature, not a flaw: it is what makes it a community commitment rather than a payout.
- **Candidate A is individual and contribution-based.** People who *helped it grow* benefit. The basis is participation, not membership.

**They are not redundant, and they are not fully compatible.** The same dollar of surplus cannot go both to a community fund and to individual contributors. **Ratifying both without a priority order commits the platform to spending the same money twice** — which is the failure mode of every well-meant revenue-sharing promise.

**The resolution is a priority order, not a merge.** Which comes first when there is less surplus than both would claim? That is a question only the PM can answer, and it should be answered before candidate A is ratified rather than discovered in the first profitable year.

**Two things would dissolve the tension, and the union steer supplies the second.**

- If candidate A means *ownership* rather than a cut of surplus, the two draw on different things and do not compete.
- **If candidate A means what a union member gets — services, bargaining power, a say — it is not a claim on money at all**, and the question stops being profit-versus-ownership. Given the PM's union framing on the same day, this is now the more likely reading and the one to test first.

## Consequences of the ownership wording — named once, no advice

**A commitment about who owns the platform constrains how it can be financed later.** Three specific places the current draft wording could close a door that was only meant to be leaned against:

- **"No shareholders" as an absolute rules out employee equity**, which is the ordinary way a small team is paid when cash is short.
- **It also rules out community or cooperative equity** — member-held shares in a co-op are still shares, and a hard no-shareholders line forbids the most member-aligned form of ownership there is.
- **It collides with candidate A.** If members share by holding equity, then members are shareholders. **A hard no-shareholders line closes the most obvious mechanism for the other candidate on the same page.**

**The word *outside* is what avoids all three.** Candidate B is now firm on both halves (upgraded 2026-09-07), so the hedge no longer does this work — the qualifier does. *No outside shareholders* closes the door to capital while leaving employee equity and member-held co-op shares available, which a union model wants. **Tighten it to bare "no shareholders" and all three doors shut.**

## The open question — promise 1 is not publishable yet

**"Above what it costs to operate" has no defensible meaning yet, and a money promise gets held to the letter.** Three things are undefined: who decides the number, over what period, and what "back into the community" concretely means. Options, with the trade-off:

- **A — Named percentage, annual, into a community fund.** Cleanest to say and to audit; commits to a specific number before there's any revenue to test it against.
- **B — Surplus defined as revenue minus operating cost and a stated founder salary, published annually.** Honest and defensible, and it makes the salary public — which is either the most credible thing on the page or the most uncomfortable.
- **C — Direction without a number: "we're not structured to extract, and we publish where the money goes."** Safe to publish today and impossible to break; also the weakest, and close enough to promise 3 that it may not earn its own line.

**Recommendation: C to launch, B as the target once there is revenue to report.** A commits to arithmetic nobody can do yet.

**Until the PM picks, promise 1 stays out of user-facing copy** and promises 2 and 3 carry the public voice alone.

## Removed

Each of these was written as a commitment. None was agreed to. All are gone from the drafts.

- **"You will never pay for visibility."** **Advertising and paid placement are not banned by fiat** — any specific mechanic passes promise 2 before it ships, and promise 2 is a real gate.
- **"The structure of the company will reflect the structure of the promise — something members own."** No decision supports it.
- **"You will leave the platform stronger than you arrived."** Survives as a description of intent, nowhere as a commitment.
- **"Your relationships with your customers belong to you — never sold, never gated."** The never-sold half follows from promise 3. The never-gated half is a product commitment nobody made.
- **"Your growth is the metric."** The measurement commitment lives in the metrics doc, and it does not say this.
- **"Free forever," "no fees, ever," "no listing fees, no transaction fees."** Money promises across three draft surfaces, all from one microcopy recipe. Replaced with what is true in the present tense: *listing costs nothing.*

## The three previously-unratified items, re-tested against the promises

- **"Reviews and star ratings are permanently deferred."** **Keep** — promise 3. A rating economy is how a platform makes itself the arbiter of who gets business, which is the extraction pattern in its most familiar form.
- **"A values statement is never sourced or inferred."** **Keep** — promise 2. A label the platform attaches to a person it can place on a map is benefit-to-product, cost-to-member, which is exactly what promise 2 forbids.
- **"Nothing ships that cannot be taken down."** **Keep, but on operational grounds, not constitutional ones.** No promise requires it. It stays because serving an image you have no way to remove is an unbounded liability with no recovery path — that is an operations argument, and it should be recorded as one rather than dressed as a principle.

## Making promise 2 a gate rather than a sentiment

Promise 2 is the only one that can be checked on a Tuesday, and right now nothing checks it. **Every decision record and every scenario needs one line: *who benefits, member and product, and what does the member give up?*** A decision that cannot fill in the member half has failed the promise, and that is the finding — not a prompt to write something reassuring in the box.

Owed: a member-benefit line in the decision-log format, in the scenario template, and in `weigh`'s close-out. Not yet done.

# How good faith is enforced

**Ratified 2026-09-07 by the PM.**

**Good faith is enforced by community and peer pressure, not by platform policing.**

> **This is not an anonymous internet platform. It is a know-and-support-your-community platform.**

That sentence is the whole model. A vendor who claims to be somewhere they are not is seen by their own followers, and the social cost lands immediately without the platform doing anything. Repercussions can be built later; **the cost exists now, because the people involved know each other.**

## What this justifies, on the merits rather than the price

**Appearances are auto-approved. Vendors declare where they will be; venue owners do not gatekeep.**

This was originally chosen because approval cost one to two days and there is no notification path to tell an owner a request is waiting. **That is a reason to defer, not a reason to prefer.** The accountability model makes it the right answer regardless of cost: an approval queue treats a false claim as something the platform must prevent, and in a place where the claimant's neighbours can see the claim, prevention is the platform doing work the community does better and faster.

**Recorded so the reasoning survives the price changing.** If approval later becomes cheap, that alone is not a reason to add it.

## Open question — the collision nobody should design around quietly

**"Let others know if a creator isn't acting in good faith" is one step from a reputation system, and reviews, ratings and reputation scores are permanently deferred under promise 3.**

These two pull against each other:

- **Community accountability needs the community to be able to say something.** A social cost that nobody can express is not a cost.
- **The refusal exists because a rating economy makes the platform the arbiter of who gets business** — which is extraction in its most familiar form, and is exactly what a five-star average becomes.

**The question, unanswered and not to be answered by whoever designs the first feature that touches it: how does community accountability work without becoming the rating economy we refused?**

**Not a launch problem.** Nothing at launch lets a member say anything about another member — the report path goes to the operator and has no public state. **But it is a design problem that will arrive**, and the first proposal that looks like a solution will look like a rating, because ratings are the obvious shape. **Whoever writes that proposal reads this section first.**
