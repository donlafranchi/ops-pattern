# 04 — Member-Owned Auto Insurance Pilot

> The first concrete vertical. Easier than health insurance — lighter regulation, well-established mutual-ownership models, ~50 mutuals still operating today. The pilot that proves the broader cooperative-insurance thesis.

---

## Practical-to-begin

Partner with an existing mutual auto insurer for a platform-branded plan in one or two cooperative-friendly states. Members access the plan through their Member status; the mutual handles underwriting, reserves, network, claims. The platform's contribution is distribution, Member-voice, and group-rate leverage. Auto insurance is dramatically easier than health: state DOIs regulate it but with far less complexity than health, MLR-style profit caps don't apply, no provider-network problem, and the mutual-ownership template has been thoroughly road-tested. State Farm, USAA, Liberty Mutual, and Nationwide all started as mutuals before some demutualized.

Six-to-twelve-month shipping looks like:

- Identify one or two mutual partners. Candidates: surviving regional mutuals (Erie Insurance, Amica Mutual, Country Financial — many still operate), or member-cooperative-aligned mutuals open to platform partnerships.
- Pilot in two states with favorable insurance regulation and strong Member density (e.g., California, Wisconsin, Vermont, Massachusetts).
- Platform's role: distribution, group-leverage on rates, Member-voice on policy and claims experience. No underwriting, no reserves, no balance-sheet exposure.
- Member value proposition: lower rates than commercial carriers in same risk pool; transparent pricing; aligned mutual ownership (Members are policy-holders are owners); aggregated Member feedback flows back to the mutual's actuarial and product teams.

The pilot is structurally simple. The platform is an infrastructure-layer producer / referrer; the mutual is the insurance entity. Existing regulatory frameworks already support this exact shape.

## Ambitious-for-the-goal

Demonstrate the pattern that proves the broader cooperative-insurance thesis. Auto today; renters, homeowners, life, dental, vision next; eventually health.

- The pilot's *real* purpose is the proof: it shows that Members can get better-priced, better-aligned coverage through a Member-owned channel without the platform itself becoming an insurance company.
- At scale, the platform partners with mutuals across every insurance vertical where mutual structures exist or can be revived. Members access the federation's full insurance shelf through their Member status: one source of coverage that is consistently Member-aligned.
- Over the longer horizon (years not months): when the federation base is large enough — meaningfully into the hundreds of thousands of Members in a single state cohort — the platform's cooperative federations could form their own member-owned mutual rather than partner with an existing one. This is the path the original ACA CO-OPs tried and mostly failed at (under-capitalized, no federation behind them). The federation layer is the difference between trying that solo and trying it with a large pre-existing cooperative base.
- Policy-advocacy layer: Member voice on profit-cap rules, repair-cost transparency, anti-collusion enforcement on body-shop networks. Members lobby through the platform; the platform stays infrastructure but provides the substrate.

## Why it serves the mandate

Auto insurance hits extraction markers 1 (profit grows with premium, though softer than health), 3 (lock-in via DMV minimum-coverage requirements), and 4 (information asymmetry on rate calculations and claims). See [`anti-extraction.md`](../../product/foundation/anti-extraction.md).

Treatments applied: realigning profit via ownership (mutual), restoring information symmetry (transparent rate explanation, Member-shared claims experience), reducing switching costs (portable Member identity across coverage types), building countervailing power (federated rate negotiation), policy advocacy (Member-voice on regulatory reform).

On the Flourishing Index: insurance is one of the largest line items in basic-needs cost. Lower premiums directly improve adequacy margin. Cooperative-aligned policies (claims paid fairly, no churn-and-burn tactics) reduce the time members spend fighting claims — discretionary hours improve.

## Open questions

- **Which mutual to partner with?** Erie, Amica, Country Financial, smaller regional mutuals — different operating areas, different Member fits. Probably evaluate two-or-three in parallel.
- **Which states first?** Insurance regulation is state-by-state; some states are dramatically friendlier than others. Start in two states with strong cooperative tradition and reasonable DOIs.
- **Differentiation vs. existing mutuals.** USAA serves veterans; State Farm has nationwide scale; Erie has loyal regional base. Why does a Member want our plan vs. those? Answer: alignment to Member-owned cooperatives across all of life's verticals, not just auto. The Member's adequacy improves across the board, not just on this one policy.
- **Revenue model.** Flat per-policy referral fee, percentage of group savings vs. open market, or other? Lean toward per-policy fee (transparent, no incentive to inflate premiums).
- **Member voice mechanics.** Aggregated Member feedback flowing back to the mutual — what's the structural form? An annual Member-voice report? Standing seats on policy review boards? Member-elected representatives to the mutual's governance?
- **Policy-advocacy posture.** Does Member-aligned advocacy come with the pilot, or stay deliberately separate to keep the partnership uncomplicated?
- **When does the federation form its own mutual?** What threshold of federated Member base justifies the leap from partnership to ownership? Probably hundreds of thousands of Members in a single state cohort, with sustained engagement and a track record on the partnership pilot.
