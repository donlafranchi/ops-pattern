# 02 — Cooperative Formation Templates

> The bridge layer. Pooled commitment proves *demand exists*. Formation templates make it possible for that demand to incorporate as a real Member-owned entity.

---

## Practical-to-begin

A curated catalog of incorporation paths per state, per cooperative kind. Partnership with one or more existing cooperative federations — NCBA CLUSA, Sustainable Economies Law Center, US Federation of Worker Cooperatives, ICA Group, Democracy at Work Institute. These organizations have already built the legal templates, model bylaws, and educator networks. The platform's contribution is the matching layer that didn't exist before: finding the 20 cleaning workers in Sacramento, the 50 parents in Burlington, the 100 broadband-deprived households in rural Wisconsin who all want the same cooperative.

Six-to-twelve-month shipping looks like:

- Pilot in three friendly states (e.g., California, Vermont, Wisconsin) covering three cooperative kinds (worker, consumer, parent / childcare).
- Pooled-Commitment threshold triggers a *Formation Track*: matched legal template, matched partner attorney, matched co-op federation educator.
- Platform tracks the resulting entity as a `kind='business'` Group with multiple owner-role memberships per the existing schema. Legal formation happens off-platform with the partner attorney.
- A "co-op-in-formation" badge surfaces on the Group page so Members can follow progress.

The platform itself never forms the entity. It scaffolds discovery, threshold, and matching.

## Ambitious-for-the-goal

Months, not years, from *"I wish there was a co-op for this"* to *"I'm an owner of one."*

- Every cooperative-friendly state runs at least one Member-led cooperative formation per quarter.
- The pattern works across all major cooperative legal vehicles: worker co-ops, consumer co-ops, parent co-ops, purchasing co-ops, L3Cs, B-corps, fraternal benefit societies, Community Land Trusts, Limited Equity Housing Cooperatives.
- Eventually the platform houses thousands of named, locally-rooted cooperatives — each its own legal entity, each owned by its Members, each visible as the Group it is.
- The biggest unlock: people who would never have thought of themselves as "cooperative founders" become founders, because the friction of forming has collapsed from "hire a lawyer and figure it out" to "tap the formation track when threshold hits."

## Why it serves the mandate

This is treatment pattern 1 in [`impact-diagnostic.md`](../../product/foundation/impact-diagnostic.md): realigning profit via ownership. The bottleneck for cooperative formation today is matching latent demand with legal infrastructure; the platform's contribution is the matching layer.

On the Flourishing Index: cooperative formation gives Members a path to *ownership stake* in services they currently consume from extractive vendors. Adequacy margin improves (members keep more of what they pay); discretionary hours improve (cooperatives often run on shorter standard work weeks than for-profits in the same vertical).

## Open questions

- **Which states first?** Friendliness varies. California (LEHC, B-corp, worker co-op statute), Vermont (L3C, well-developed co-op tradition), Wisconsin (worker co-op statute, ag co-op history), Minnesota (farmer co-op tradition), Massachusetts (CLT-friendly municipalities), Oregon (co-op statute), Maryland (B-corp). Start in three; expand based on demand.
- **Which federations to partner with?** NCBA CLUSA has the broadest reach; Sustainable Economies Law Center has the deepest legal-template library; US Federation of Worker Cooperatives is the largest worker-co-op network. Probably partner with two-or-three rather than picking one.
- **What threshold of interest triggers a formation track?** Fixed (e.g., 20 committed Members), or location-density-dependent (e.g., 10 within 5 miles)? Probably varies by cooperative kind.
- **Platform liability.** What does liability look like when the platform scaffolds entity formation? Pure referral (lowest exposure), some level of supervision (higher exposure but better quality control), or platform-as-formation-agent (high exposure, probably wrong). Lean toward pure referral.
- **How does this interact with Pooled Commitment (01)?** Probably: pooled commitment is the *signal* that a formation track should activate. Threshold met → formation offer surfaces to the committers.
- **What happens when a cooperative fails?** The platform should not be in the business of saving failing cooperatives; that's a Member-vote decision. But the platform should make it cheap to *dissolve cleanly* — the no-impersonal-Business commitment cuts both ways.
