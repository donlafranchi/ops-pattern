# 03 — Federation Layer

> Where the leverage emerges. Pooled commitment proves demand; formation creates the entity; federation is what gives those entities the scale to negotiate, share services, and shift markets.

---

## Practical-to-begin

When two or more cooperatives on the platform are doing similar work, they can opt to federate. The federation is itself a `kind='business'` Group whose Members are *other Groups* — a structural variant of the existing schema where group-of-groups is allowed. Federation members pay small fees to fund shared services; surplus distributes back to member-cooperatives per patronage (the volume of services consumed determines the share of returned surplus).

Six-to-twelve-month shipping looks like:

- Two pilot federations: a small federation of cleaning cooperatives across three California cities; a federation of childcare cooperatives across Vermont.
- Initial shared services: joint supplier negotiation (one platform, dozens of co-ops buying together), shared bookkeeping pattern (the same off-platform accounting firm serving multiple co-ops at a federated rate), shared legal counsel retainer (the same co-op-friendly law firm available to all federation members).
- Governance pattern: one-cooperative-one-vote on shared-service decisions; volume-weighted on patronage distribution.
- Platform's role: infrastructure only — the schema, the Member discovery, the visibility. Federations run themselves.

## Ambitious-for-the-goal

The Ace Hardware analog applied across every cooperative vertical, federated bottom-up rather than franchised top-down.

- A federation of cleaning cooperatives across 50 cities, negotiating jointly with cleaning-supplies vendors. Each city's cooperative remains its own entity; the federation gives them Costco-scale purchasing.
- A federation of childcare cooperatives across a state, sharing curriculum, regulatory compliance, and an HR back-office. Each cooperative still runs itself locally; the federation removes the overhead that crushes most small co-ops.
- A federation of farmer cooperatives shipping nationally. Each farm cooperative remains rooted in its region; the federation handles logistics, food-safety compliance, and joint sales to large institutional buyers.
- Federations of federations. All worker cooperatives across all verticals federating jointly to negotiate a Member-owned mutual health plan. A federation of housing cooperatives jointly procuring solar installation. A federation of repair cooperatives advocating for right-to-repair laws.
- Eventually, the federation graph becomes the platform's organizing principle. The platform is no longer a directory of individuals plus a directory of businesses — it's a directory of Members nested in cooperatives nested in federations, with leverage compounding at each level.

This is the structural answer to the "Costco does insurance" question from earlier. Federation captures the same scale leverage without the legal contortion of consumer-employer status, and without the single-attack-surface of a subsidiary holding company.

## Why it serves the mandate

This is treatment pattern 4 in [`anti-extraction.md`](../../product/foundation/anti-extraction.md) at full strength: countervailing power assembled bottom-up. Federation captures the leverage of consolidation *without the extraction of consolidation*, because surplus distributes back to member-cooperatives and members rather than to external shareholders.

This is also where the platform's revenue model anchors. A small federation service fee — capped by Member vote, with hard ceilings written into the structural form — sustains the platform infrastructure. Anchored to the patronage-dividend pattern that cooperatives already accept, never to engagement metrics.

On the Flourishing Index: federation gives Members access to bulk pricing, shared services, joint advocacy, and joint legal action without sacrificing the local-rootedness of their cooperative. Adequacy improves (better prices, lower overhead); discretionary hours improve (shared back-office removes operational toil).

## Open questions

- **Schema.** Is "Group of Groups" the right model, or does federation deserve a new primitive? Probably the former — keep the schema flat; let federation be an emergent property of Groups whose members are Groups.
- **Governance.** One Member one vote? One cooperative one vote? Weighted by membership size? Different voting rules for different decisions (e.g., service-purchasing weighted by usage; bylaws one-cooperative-one-vote)?
- **Platform's role.** Pure infrastructure (federation runs itself entirely off-platform), or some level of facilitation (platform manages shared service contracts on behalf of the federation)? Lean toward the former; the platform stays infrastructure.
- **Conflict with no-impersonal-Business commitment.** Federations are not corporate shells but they are not single-Person either. The principle holds because every member of the federation is itself a Group whose Members are named Persons — impersonality has nowhere to hide.
- **Anti-extraction inside federations.** What prevents a large cooperative from dominating a federation and extracting from smaller ones? Probably: voting rules that protect minority cooperatives, transparent patronage-dividend formulas, and easy exit (any member-cooperative can leave the federation at any time without losing its standalone status).
- **Revenue capping.** What's the structural mechanism that prevents the platform's federation-service fees from growing into extraction? Probably: federation members vote on platform fee ceilings annually; any increase requires supermajority of cooperatives.
