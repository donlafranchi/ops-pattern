## 1. Farmers Market Scene

### Dane County Farmers' Market (DCFM) — The Anchor

America's largest producer-only farmers' market. Founded 1972 by Mayor Bill Dyke. Organized as a **vendor-run cooperative** with a 9-member elected Board of Directors (all vendor-members). Market Manager: **Jamie Bugel** (since ~2021; MS in agroecology, UW-Madison). Assistant Manager: **Jill Carlson Groendyk**.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total vendor-members | ~275 throughout the year |
| Peak Saturday vendors | 150-170, completely encircling the State Capitol |
| Vendor composition | 82% farmers, 18% cheesemakers/bakers/confectioners |
| Peak Saturday attendance | 20,000+ (post-COVID surge; pre-pandemic ~15,000) |
| Annual consumer spending | ~$10 million |
| Annual spillover to nearby businesses | ~$6 million |
| Waiting list for new vendors | 1-2 years (historically up to 5) |

**Strict producer-only rule**: all items must be Wisconsin-grown/raised/produced by the person behind the stand. No resale. No crafts. This is a cultural value, not just policy.

**Year-round operation across four formats:**

| Format | Season | Location | Vendors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saturday on the Square | Apr 11 – Nov 14 | Capitol Square | 150-170 |
| Wednesday Market | Apr 22 – Nov 4 | 200 block MLK Jr. Blvd | ~30 |
| Holiday Market | Nov–Dec | Monona Terrace | TBD |
| Winter Market | Jan 3 – Apr 4 | Garver Feed Mill | 50+ |

SNAP/EBT accepted at every market. **Double Dollars program** matches SNAP spending dollar-for-dollar up to $20 (city/county funded). Nine Dane County markets participate.

**Key detail for platform design:** Saturday on the Square is actually four events in one — DCFM vendors (state property, Capitol grounds), nonprofit/political info booths (interior Capitol grounds), arts & crafts + concessions (city property across the street), and street musicians. Different permits, different jurisdictions.

## 2. Local Business / Maker Ecosystem

### Independent Business Density

Madison's indie scene is concentrated in distinct corridors, most with formal business associations:

- **State Street / Capitol Square (Central BID):** 350+ shops, galleries, restaurants. 82% locally owned. Formed 1999.
- **Williamson Street ("Willy Street"):** Anchored by Willy Street Co-op. Signature indie district.
- **Monroe Street:** 1.5+ miles of locally-owned businesses. Monthly First Fridays, annual Monroe Street Festival. Merchants association is 48 years old.
- **Atwood Avenue (SASY neighborhood):** Transformed blue-collar corridor. Most owners live nearby.
- **Northside, South Park Street, Regent:** Each with its own business association.

The City of Madison formally supports **9+ Neighborhood Business Associations** through its Economic Development division.

### Maker Spaces

**Sector67** (56 Corry St) — 19,320 sq ft nonprofit. 3D printers, laser cutters, CNC, woodshop, electronics. $100/month ($50 students). Also runs Madison Tool Library.

**The Bodgery** (740 Oscar Ave, former Oscar Mayer warehouse) — 21,000+ sq ft. Wood, metal, CNC, glass, leather, screen printing, welding. $50/month, 24/7 access. Open houses 1st and 3rd Fridays.

**UW-Madison Design Innovation Lab** — 25,000+ sq ft. Open to students/faculty; fee-for-service for others.

### Artisan Events (year-round calendar)

- **Art Fair on the Square** (MMoCA): 400+ artists, ~200,000 visitors
- **Madison Night Market**: 100+ vendors on State Street, 5 Thursday evenings May–Oct (9th year in 2026)
- **Madison Makers Market**: Pop-up markets, intentionally 40-60% new makers per event, at least one nonprofit vendor
- **Winter Art Fair Off the Square** (MMoCA): 135+ Wisconsin artists
- **Gallery Night**: Semi-annual city-wide gallery crawl
- **Marquette-Atwood Art Walk**: 60 artists at 39 residential locations
- **Fete de Marquette**: 100,000+ attendees, local vendor marketplace

### Buy-Local Infrastructure

**Dane Buy Local** — the critical organization. Founded 2004. 500+ member businesses. 30,000-copy printed membership guide. Monthly networking (100+ attendees). Administered **$28M in COVID grants to 2,700+ small businesses**. Executive Director: Paula Severson.

Key stat from Civic Economics study: Dane County independents recirculate **60.7% of revenue locally vs. 30.4% for chains** — a 2:1 ratio. Shifting 10% of spending from chains to local would retain **$173-235 million annually** in the local economy.

### Madison Public Market — Opening July 23, 2026

$13.2M facility at 202 N. First Street. ~130 local businesses, food entrepreneur incubation center (TruStage MarketReady Hall). Projected: 500,000+ annual visitors, 100 jobs, $10M in local sales. **No digital coordination layer announced.** Timing alignment for a platform launch.

### Connector Organizations

- **Dane Buy Local** — 500+ businesses, the primary buy-local coalition
- **Capital Entrepreneurs** — founded 2009, monthly meetups, annual Founder Day
- **Doyenne Group** — women entrepreneurs, professional development + direct funding
- **WWBIC** — SBA-funded Women's Business Center, education + loans
- **MadREP** — 8-county economic development agency, 90+ development professionals
- **Greater Madison Chamber** — 1,200+ members, Kiva 0% loans, SBER grants

## 3. Community Organizations

### Cooperative Culture (nationally exceptional)

Madison has **75-80+ cooperatives** including **14 worker-owned cooperatives employing 500+ people** — the highest rate of worker-cooperative employment per capita in the country.

**Willy Street Co-op:** Founded 1974. **34,000+ member-owners** (12% of city population). $69.5M gross revenue. 385 employees. 3 locations. 180+ local farmers/vendors. Community CHIP program has raised $5M+ for nonprofits since 1978.

**Municipal investment:** In 2014, Madison's Common Council approved **$5 million for worker cooperative development** ($1M/year, 2016-2020) — **the largest such allocation by any U.S. municipality**. First-year results: 21 new cooperatives launched, 141 workers gained employment and ownership.

**Named worker co-ops:** Union Cab (est. 1979, 400K rides/year), Community Pharmacy (est. 1972, 20 worker-owners), Isthmus Engineering (~80 employees, $30M+ sales), Just Coffee (B-Corp), Interpreters' Cooperative (50+ languages), Tone Madison (journalism co-op), Roots4Change (first Dane County co-op led by Latina/indigenous women).

**Infrastructure:**
- **UW Center for Cooperatives (UWCC):** Founded 1962 — only U.S. university-based center studying all forms of co-op business. Maintains Wisconsin Co-op Directory (728 co-ops statewide).
- **Cooperative Network:** HQ'd in Madison — largest statewide co-op trade association in the U.S., ~400 member co-ops.
- **Madison Cooperative Development Coalition (MCDC):** City-funded, targeting communities of color with $10K grants requiring no formal business plan.
- **MadWorC:** Peer network of 10+ worker co-ops.
- **Credit unions:** Summit CU (280K members, $8.6B assets) + UW CU (386K members, $6.3B assets) = 660,000+ members, ~$14.9B combined assets.

### Neighborhood Associations

**120+ officially recognized** — one of the most densely organized cities of its size in the country. City maintains an electronic directory with contacts, meeting schedules, boundaries. GIS dataset on Open Data portal.

**City support infrastructure:**
- Neighborhood Grant Program (~$25K/year in small grants)
- 9 Neighborhood Resource Teams (est. 1991) serving specific low-income neighborhoods
- 15 city-funded neighborhood centers (Goodman, Wil-Mar, Lussier, Kennedy Heights, etc.)
- 3 Planning Councils (Northside, Eastside, South Metropolitan) — coalitions of associations

Notable: Marquette Neighborhood Association (founded 1968, runs Orton Park Festival, Willy Street Fair). SASY (Schenk-Atwood-Starkweather-Yahara, runs AtwoodFest). Tenney-Lapham (Taste of Tenney).

### Community Development Organizations

- **Urban League of Greater Madison:** Founded 1968. Operates the **Black Business Hub** (2352 S. Park St.) — premier enterprise center for BIPOC entrepreneurs.
- **Centro Hispano of Dane County:** Founded 1983. New $6.85M "Calli" facility (2024). Operates **Mercadito** weekly community market for immigrant vendors.
- **YWCA Madison:** Chartered 1909, Capitol Square location.
- **Freedom, Inc.:** Queer Black and Southeast Asian feminist org — voter education, organizing.
- **Bayview Foundation:** Affordable housing, ~50% Hmong residents, 12+ languages spoken.
- **Social Justice Center:** Houses 14 member organizations including MadWorC, Progressive Dane, IWW-Madison, Family Farm Defenders.

### Mutual Aid

- **Madison Mutual Aid Network (MAN) Cooperative:** Timebanking, Everywhere Gardens (matches land with gardeners), shared savings pools, emergency funds.
- **Dane County TimeBank:** **2,500+ individuals and organizations.** Youth Court in all four high schools, Medical Transportation, Front Yard Gardens, prisoner reentry timebanking.

### Community Land Trusts

- **Madison Area Community Land Trust (MACLT):** Founded 1991. 60+ permanently affordable homes. **Troy Gardens:** 31-acre project — 30 cohousing homes, 5-acre organic farm, 327 community garden plots.

## 4. Local Food & Farm Networks

### CSA Programs

**FairShare CSA Coalition** — 501(c)(3) at 211 S Paterson St. Coalition of **30+ CSA farms**. Runs Partner Shares Program (financial assistance for low-income households) and an Organic Vegetable Farm Manager Apprenticeship.

### Food Hubs

**REAP Food Group** (306 E Wilson St) — Founded 1997. Key programs:
- **Farm Fresh Atlas**: 50,000 copies/year statewide, distributed to every library in Wisconsin. 400+ farms listed. This is literally an analog local discovery platform.
- Farm to School (scratch cooking pilot with Madison Metro School District)
- Farm to Business (connecting farms to institutional buyers)
- Part of **Foodshed Partnership** (with Community Action Coalition, Pasture and Plenty, FairShare) to open a food hub

**Wisconsin Food Hub Cooperative** — Farmer-led co-op, 50+ farm members, 9,000 sq ft refrigerated warehouse. $250,000 USDA grant (2026) to expand school vegetable supply.

**FEED Kitchens** (Northside Planning Council) — Incubates **80+ food businesses** (70% people of color, 50% women-owned).

**Rooted** — Manages 70 community gardens serving 2,000 families.

### Farm-to-Table Recognition

Madison's farm-to-table scene **predates the national trend**:
- **L'Etoile:** Founded 1976 by Odessa Piper. Now run by Tory Miller (James Beard Best Chef Midwest 2012).
- **Fairchild:** Owners Itaru Nagano and Andrew Kroeger won James Beard Best Chef Midwest 2023.
- **Pasture and Plenty:** James Beard nominee 2024.
- **Top Chef: Wisconsin** (Season 21) filmed in Madison 2023-2024.
- **Edible Madison** won the inaugural James Beard Publication of the Year Award (2011).

### Agricultural Scale

Dane County is **#1 in Wisconsin** for agricultural market value ($509 million) with **2,564 farms**.

## 5. Tech / Startup Scene

### Incubators & Accelerators

**StartingBlock Madison** — 50,000 sq ft hub at 821 E Washington Ave. Built on $10M capital campaign. Houses Wisconsin HardTech Accelerator, 1 Million Cups, Her Network, Startup Career Fair.

**gener8tor** — Nationally operating accelerator HQ'd in Madison/Milwaukee. **180+ portfolio companies have raised $1B+ in follow-on financing.** Three Madison programs: Investment Accelerator ($100K/cohort), gBETA (free 7-week pre-accelerator), gALPHA (student venture creation).

**100state** — Madison's largest coworking community (150+ members), nonprofit, at 17 S Fairchild St.

### UW-Madison Innovation Engine

- **Discovery to Product (D2P):** ~400 innovators and ~200 projects annually. Helped launch 150+ startups. $1.4M+ in annual grants.
- **University Research Park:** 37 buildings, 1.8M+ sq ft, ~125 companies, 3,500-4,000 employees. Forbes: one of "10 especially crackling innovation hubs" worldwide.
- **WARF (Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation):** Nation's first university tech transfer org (founded 1925). ~4,400 patents issued. $1.2B cumulative licensing revenue. $3.1B endowment. 200+ startups formed on WARF technologies.
- **PitchBook ranking:** UW-Madison #6 Public US-Undergrad and #9 Public US-Graduate for VC-backed entrepreneur alumni.

### Early Adopter Indicators

- **Brookings/ITIF ranked Madison #1 out of 35 cities** with potential to become innovation hubs (2019). "We need 10 more Madisons."
- 71 patents per 100,000 population
- #1 STEM employment density nationally (10,554 per 100K)
- 700+ tech companies, 24,000+ tech jobs
- 47% tech labor market growth 2014-2019
- Highest millennial concentration in the US: 26.8% of population
- Classified as an "emerging AI hub"

### Notable Companies

Epic Systems (Verona, ~13,000 employees, ~$5B revenue), Exact Sciences (Cologuard, WARF spinout), Promega, Fetch ($631M+ funding, 55M+ downloads), Propeller Health (acquired for $225M). Microsoft and Google maintain R&D labs in Madison.

### Tech Community

- **Forward Fest:** Wisconsin's largest tech/entrepreneurship festival — 5,000+ attendees, 40+ events over 5 days (August)
- **Madison Women in Tech:** 2,500+ members
- **Capital Entrepreneurs:** Monthly meetups since 2009
- **Data Science Madison, MadAI, Madison Area Software Developers**
- **Madison Startups** (madisonstartups.com): Weekly event aggregator

## 8. Existing Competition & Similar Tools

### Core Finding: No Unified Local Discovery Platform Exists

The landscape is fragmented across national platforms with local presence and media-owned directories. No single tool combines events + businesses + community + marketplace + people.

| Platform | What It Does | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| **DCFM** (dcfm.org) | Largest producer-only market in the U.S. 275 vendors. | **No app.** Basic informational website. No digital marketplace layer. |
| **Farm Fresh Atlas** (REAP) | 400+ farms statewide. 50,000 print copies/year to every WI library. | **Literally an analog discovery platform.** The digital equivalent is what we're building. |
| **FairShare CSA Coalition** | 50+ CSA farms, interactive farm search. | CSA-specific only. |
| **Dane Buy Local** | 500+ business directory, 30K-copy printed guide. | Advocacy org, not consumer app. Directory is static. |
| **Isthmus** (isthmus.com) | Richest structured event discovery in Madison. Map-based restaurant guide. | Events + dining only. No marketplace, community, or member profiles. |
| **Visit Madison** | Tourism bureau. Event calendar, business listings. | Tourism-oriented, not resident-focused. |
| **Cap Times** | Broadest event calendar (25+ categories). | News platform with calendar bolt-on. |
| **Goodie Bag** | Surplus food marketplace. Launched Madison Feb 2025. | Food surplus only — narrow scope. |
| **608today** | Daily newsletter + community event calendar (6AM City network). | Newsletter-first, not structured discovery. |
| **City Cast Madison** | Daily podcast + newsletter (17,500+ subscribers). | Audio/newsletter, not platform. |
| **Nextdoor** | Covers dozens of Madison neighborhoods. ~50-80K accounts. | Neighborhood-siloed. No cross-neighborhood discovery, events, or marketplace. |
| **Facebook — Madison Foodie** | ~50,000 members, ~80 posts/day. | Food recommendations only, locked in Facebook. |
| **Facebook — Buy/Sell/Trade** | 8-10 overlapping groups with no dominant one. | Classic coordination failure. Fragmented. |
| **Facebook — Dane County Neighbors Helping Neighbors** | 15,000+ members. COVID-born mutual aid. | Locked inside Facebook. |
| **r/madisonwi** | ~34,570 subscribers. Active "where do I find X" threads. | Reddit UX, not purpose-built. |
| **Dane County TimeBank** | 2,500+ individuals. | Basic website, not mobile-first. |
| **Foraged** (foraged.com) | Order from local farmers markets for pickup. | Markets only. |

### Structural Gaps

1. **No unified local discovery** — nothing combines events + businesses + community + marketplace
2. **No farmers' market digital layer** — the largest producer-only market in the U.S. has no app
3. **No cross-neighborhood view** — Nextdoor silos by neighborhood, Facebook by topic
4. **No people-first commerce** — everything is business-directory-shaped or classified-ad-shaped
5. **No physical-to-digital bridge** — Madison Public Market (July 2026) launching with no digital layer
6. **Mutual aid trapped in Facebook** — 15K+ in Facebook groups, 2,500+ in timebank, no unified tool
7. **Events scattered** across 5-7 calendars with no aggregator
8. **Buy/sell fragmented** across 8-10 Facebook groups

## 9. Champion Profiles

### Champion 1: Farmers' Market Organizer / Vendor Board Member

**Why:** DCFM is a cooperative of 275 vendors with an elected board. 20,000+ weekly visitors. No app, no pre-ordering, no digital vendor discovery. The cooperative governance means the board can make adoption decisions. 82% of vending businesses are farms.

**Entry point:** DCFM Board of Directors (9 vendor-members), Market Manager Jamie Bugel, Westside Community Market Manager Ben Zimmerman.

**What they'd champion:** Vendor profiles, product discovery, market-day coordination, cross-market visibility across 12+ markets.

### Champion 2: Co-op Community Engagement Leader

**Why:** Willy Street Co-op's 34,000 member-owners is the single largest organized consumer base in the city — 12% of the population. They source from 180+ local farmers/vendors. Community CHIP raised $5M+ for nonprofits. MadWorC coordinates 10+ worker co-ops already thinking in terms of mutual aid and local economic circulation.

**Entry point:** Willy Street Co-op Board, community engagement staff, MadWorC member co-ops.

**What they'd champion:** Local product discovery, producer visibility, cooperative values alignment, member engagement beyond the grocery transaction.

### Champion 3: Buy-Local Advocate / Business District Leader

**Why:** Dane Buy Local has 500+ member businesses self-identifying as locally owned. They publish a 30,000-copy printed guide — a guide that wants to become a living digital platform. Nine neighborhood business associations cover distinct corridors.

**Entry point:** Dane Buy Local (Executive Director Paula Severson), Downtown Madison BID (370+ businesses), Monroe Street Merchants, Atwood/SASY merchants.

**What they'd champion:** Business discovery, local-first purchasing, district-level coordination, replacing the analog printed guide.

### Champion 4: Food System Connector

**Why:** REAP's Farm Fresh Atlas (50,000 copies/year to every WI library) is literally an analog version of what we're building. FairShare coordinates 50+ CSAs. FEED Kitchens incubates 80+ food businesses (70% people of color). These organizations have the supply-side relationships already mapped.

**Entry point:** REAP Food Group, FairShare CSA Coalition, FEED Kitchens / Northside Planning Council.

**What they'd champion:** Farm-to-consumer discovery, CSA visibility, food entrepreneur launch platform, equity-centered food access.

### Champion 5: Neighborhood Association Connector / Community Center Director

**Why:** 120+ formally recognized neighborhood associations. 15+ city-funded community centers. 3 Planning Councils. This is the hyperlocal infrastructure layer — physical gathering points in every part of the city where a platform could be introduced neighborhood by neighborhood.

**Entry point:** Capitol Neighborhoods Inc. (has paid staff), Marquette Neighborhood Association, Goodman Community Center, Planning Councils.

**What they'd champion:** Neighborhood-level event discovery, cross-neighborhood visibility, community coordination, mutual aid, bridging 120+ associations into a shared platform.

## Bottom Line

Madison is not just a plausible seed market — it may be the strongest seed market in the United States for this platform. Three pillars:

**1. Pre-organized supply side.** 275 DCFM vendors, 34,000 co-op member-owners, 500+ Dane Buy Local businesses, 50+ CSA farms, 80+ FEED Kitchens food entrepreneurs, 9+ business district associations. These are not atomized individuals — they are organized networks with governance structures, communication channels, and shared values.

**2. No incumbent.** The largest producer-only market in the country has no app. The most prominent analog discovery tool (Farm Fresh Atlas, 50K copies) is a printed guide. Events are scattered across 5-7 calendars. Buy/sell is fragmented across 8-10 Facebook groups. The gap is structural and visible.

**3. Cultural alignment.** A city that invested $5M in cooperative development, where 91% show up to vote, where 12% of the population are co-op member-owners, where 82% of downtown businesses are locally owned, and where the progressive tradition traces to 1900. This population already believes in collective local economic action. The platform doesn't have to convince them of the value proposition. It has to give them a tool.

**Timing:** The Madison Public Market opens July 23, 2026 — a new physical anchor for local commerce launching with no announced digital coordination layer.
