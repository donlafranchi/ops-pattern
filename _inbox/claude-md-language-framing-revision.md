## Language & Framing

The platform makes an argument about how prosperity gets created and kept. Vocabulary is scoped to the channel, because the same word does different work depending on whether the reader chose to hear it.

### The channels

| Channel | Vocabulary | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Product surfaces** — in-app copy, CTAs, onboarding, listings, empty states | No ideological vocabulary at all. Plain verbs for plain actions. | Someone listing sourdough does not need a thesis. The first rep stays frictionless. |
| **Producer & market recruitment** — vendor pitches, market-manager outreach, seed-market materials | Pro-competition, anti-extraction, keep-dollars-close. Free enterprise, small operator, fair shot, ownership. | This audience has not opted in. They are deciding whether the platform takes a cut. Free enterprise polls at 81%; the contested word polls at 39%. Use the language that is already true and already welcome. |
| **Manifesto layer** — the thesis page, about, the movement front door, merchandise | The full argument, including the contested word, defined on the platform's own terms. | This reader clicked through to hear it. Withholding the argument here wastes the one place it is invited. |
| **The name** | Carries the signal permanently, in every channel. | Acknowledged, not scoped. This is exactly why the other three rows are scoped tightly. |

### The definitional rule

Wherever a contested word appears, it arrives **already defined, by a mechanism the reader can check** — member-owned, member-governed, wealth circulating rather than extracted, every action traceable to a named human. Never by an abstraction the reader has to fill in themselves.

Americans define socialism inconsistently; the word functions as a container that the listener fills from their own priors. A term left undefined does not land neutrally — it lands as whatever the reader already believed. Defining it in the same breath is not a hedge; it is the entire mechanism by which the meaning moves.

### Demonstrate first, name second

Opponents of federal programs called the Interstate Highway System socialism. They called the polio vaccine socialism. Neither won its argument semantically. Both won by working, publicly, where people could see them — and the label lost its force afterward, because it had been stuck to something that visibly worked.

The order holds here. A member who kept $400 in their own town this quarter is the argument. The word is what that argument gets called once it is undeniable.

### Still out, in every channel

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Oligarchy / corporate greed | Rigged market / market consolidation |
| Resist | Take back / reclaim |
| Ethical spending | Smart spending / voting with your wallet |
| Progressive / the left / any party or movement identifier | *(no substitute — cut it)* |

Team-signaling is a separate thing from the argument, and it costs more than it returns. Reclaiming a contested *idea* invites people to reconsider something. Recruiting for a contested *team* asks them to switch sides, and forecloses the audience the first job needs. The platform does the first and not the second.

No villain-naming. No doom framing. No scarcity framing. No scolding register. The voice stays cheeky-defiant rather than aggrieved — the people running this are not broken by what they are working against, and the copy should sound like it.

**Audience: for all Americans.** Unchanged, and load-bearing. Any copy that reads as written for one half of the country has failed this section regardless of which words it used.
