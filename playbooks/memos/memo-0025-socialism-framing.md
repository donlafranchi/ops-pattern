# Memo 0025 — Scope the word, don't ban it

> **Status: proposed, not ratified.** Written at PM direction. `weigh` has not run. Nothing downstream should cite this until the State tag reads Ratified.

## What is being reversed

The prior rule was a blanket avoidance table. Every ideological term translated to a market-populist substitute; "anti-capitalist / progressive values" translated to "pro-competition / American values"; the stated audience was "for all Americans." The rule was global — it applied identically to a product CTA, a vendor pitch, and a manifesto page.

That rule is now inconsistent with a decision the PM has made: the platform is named SocialUs, and the name is doing deliberate work. The stated hypothesis is that Americans do not object to socialism as such, but to a version of it they were taught — and that a working demonstration can change what the word calls to mind.

Two rules cannot both be in force. The blanket table would forbid the platform from ever making the argument its own name announces.

## What the evidence supports, and where it stops

The hypothesis is partly corroborated. Gallup's own analysis finds Americans define socialism inconsistently — government control of the economy, a softer communism, a restriction on freedom, or simply equality and public provision, depending on who is asked. Roughly a fifth of Americans hold favorable views of socialism *and* capitalism simultaneously, which is not a coherent position about two rival systems; it is what a loose container looks like. Opponents of federal programs have historically applied the label to the Interstate Highway System and to the polio vaccine. The word is a container, not a concept, and what fills it is supplied by the listener.

That is the strongest available support for the PM's position, and it is real support.

It also names the constraint. Because the listener fills the container, a *name* does not get to define the term — the listener's priors do, in the half-second before any argument arrives. Which is why the word is an efficient way to pick a fight and an inefficient way to explain one. The current numbers: free enterprise 81% favorable, capitalism 54%, socialism 39%; a majority of Republicans say there is nothing good about socialism at all. Two of the three seed markets under research, Boise and Savannah, sit in states where that is the majority local read.

The polio precedent carries the sequencing lesson as well as the grievance. The vaccine did not win by winning the semantic argument first. It won by working, publicly, in front of people who could see it — and the label lost its sting afterward, because it had been attached to something that visibly worked. Vaccine confidence today is likewise recovered through trusted local messengers and demonstrated outcomes, not by leading with the contested word. Demonstration first, name second, is not a retreat from the argument. It is the order in which the argument has historically been won.

## The decision

**Decision.** Ideological vocabulary is scoped by channel rather than banned platform-wide: absent from product surfaces and producer recruitment, permitted and defined on the platform's own terms in the manifesto layer that people opt into.

**Intent.** The blanket ban made the platform's own name unspeakable in its own copy, which is untenable. Lifting the ban entirely would put a contested word in front of the exact audience the platform must recruit first — market vendors and market managers in Boise and Savannah — before any demonstration exists to give it meaning. Channel scoping keeps the argument fully available where the audience has chosen to hear it, and keeps the first rep frictionless where they have not. The test for any future copy: has this reader opted into the argument, or are they here to list a thing they made? *(Deferred until first seed market reaches 100 active Members; review by 2027-06-30.)*

## What does not change

- No villain-naming, no doom framing, no scarcity framing, no scolding register, in any channel.
- Team-signaling vocabulary — "progressive," "the left," "comrades," party or movement identifiers — stays out everywhere, including the manifesto layer. Reclaiming a contested *idea* and recruiting for a contested *team* are different jobs; the second one forecloses the audience the first one needs.
- "For all Americans" stands as the audience definition.
- The word never appears undefined. Wherever it is used, it arrives already defined by a mechanism a reader can check — member-owned, member-governed, wealth circulating rather than extracted — never by an abstraction the reader has to fill in.

## Open questions for `weigh`

1. Does the name itself constitute a permanent Layer Two use of the word that the channel scoping cannot contain? If it does, the scoping rule mitigates rather than resolves the tension, and that should be stated plainly rather than papered over.
2. Is the manifesto layer served by the `.org` front door, a `/thesis` route on the main domain, or a separate property? The channel rule assumes a distinct surface exists.
3. What evidence would reverse this memo? Proposed trigger: if seed-market vendor recruitment in Boise or Savannah underperforms Madison by a material margin on first-contact conversion, the name is filtering before the product speaks, and the scoping rule is not enough.
