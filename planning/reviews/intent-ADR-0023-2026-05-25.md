## Statements considered (and why not flagged)

### a. ADR-0023 Decision §2 — Category 2 / Category 3 (URL-omitted tier)

> "the rendered URL **omits `county`-kind segments** … A `county` segment appears in a URL only when the leaf place has no `city` ancestor … or as a collision disambiguator."

**Why not flagged.** Intent annotation #1 is substantive and audit-voiced: anchors the schema/URL split in failure modes (coverage at the data layer; legibility + name-repeat avoidance at the URL layer) and gives the tie-break rule (re-introduce county segment only for cityless leaves or as disambiguator). A downstream agent has the disposition rule for cases the prose doesn't cover.

### b. ADR-0023 Decision §3 — Category 5 (uniqueness scope change)

> "a `city` slug must be unique within its `state` ancestor … This amends ADR-0020's `places UNIQUE (parent_id, slug)` for `city` rows"

**Why not flagged.** Inline rationale immediately precedes ("Because the county is omitted from the path, `/p/ca/{city}` must resolve unambiguously") and the trade-offs section names the empirical fact that backs the constraint and the disambiguator path. Substantive and tie-breakable.

### c. ADR-0023 Decision §1 — Category 3 (slug ≠ display_name split)

> "California → ca, Oregon → or. `display_name` keeps the full name for UI."

**Why not flagged.** Intent annotation #2 covers the split substantively (USPS codes universal/stable/unambiguous on the URL side; full name preserved in `display_name` for UI). Matches the durable schema-vs-UI rule in `CLAUDE.md` § Naming conventions.

### d. ADR-0023 Consequences — Category 7 (cross-doc amendments)

> "Amends ADR-0020 (URL rendering and city slug-uniqueness scope) … Refines ADR-0022 (the `county` tier ADR-0022 introduced is made URL-transparent)."

**Why not flagged.** Local contribution is named at each citation (which ADR-0020 clause is amended; what ADR-0022's tier loses and keeps). One-step traceability to the prior ADRs preserved.
