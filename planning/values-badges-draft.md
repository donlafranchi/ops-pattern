# Values badge categories — research for SocialUs

Researched 2026-10-01. Web sources listed at the end. Marked **[unverified]** where I could not confirm from a fetched page.

SocialUs rules this research assumes:
- Values tags are private, never shown, never searchable.
- A Page owner may opt in to show a **badge** (or banner). Each badge is a high-level **category** that many values tags roll up into.
- Owner claims use "Says …" wording (e.g. "Says locally owned").
- Members rank purchasing priorities (e.g. price, eco-responsible, fair trade, employee care).

## 2. Identity-based attributes

**How platforms do it**
- Yelp (from June 2020) and Google: owner opt-in, self-reported, no verification, **searchable**, shown on the listing (Yelp shows an icon next to the name in results).
- Faire: separate "Community" filters, also self-reported.
- Discovery is the stated purpose: Yelp reported LGBTQ+-owned searches up 150%+ year over year.

**Documented problems**
- **Rating drop after tagging.** Bui (U. Michigan) & Moy (U. Penn), 250K+ Yelp reviews: after the Black-owned tag, Detroit Black-owned restaurants' average slipped 3.91 → 3.88 while others held at 3.90; reviewers who mentioned knowing the ownership averaged **3.03 stars vs 3.78**. The tag brought a small temporary spike in reviews, not lasting business. Effect varies by city — "not one-size-fits-all."
- **Hate reviews.** Yelp removed 475 anti-Asian hate reviews in 2022 vs 9 in 2021 (26,500 hate removals overall). Yelp responded by proactively screening reviews on identity-tagged pages *before* posting.
- **Review-bombing from news.** Yelp's 2020 racism alerts were partly a response to floods of reviews from people reacting to news, not visits — a pile-on mechanism that can turn on any visibly labeled business.
- **Coordinated campaigns.** 2023 anti-LGBTQ+ campaigns hit brands seen as LGBTQ+-friendly with bomb threats, boycotts and in-store confrontations (SPLC and HRC called them coordinated).
- **Backlash to the label itself.** Google's Black-owned label drew criticism that labeling owners by race echoed segregation-era practices, and that it could cause some to avoid the business.

**How this fits SocialUs's rules**
- SocialUs already removes the biggest attack surface: values can't be searched, so nobody can build a list of "all X-owned Pages."
- It also removes the main benefit platforms claim — search and discovery. A badge shown only on the Page itself still exposes the owner to anyone who visits.
- Identity is a fact about a person, not a practice. Every other proposed category is something a business *does*.

**Question for Don (not a decision)**
- A — No identity badges. Values tags may still hold them privately. Trade-off: cleanest fit with "practices, not people"; some owners want to say it publicly and can still do so in their own Page text.
- B — One opt-in identity badge category ("Says owned by …", owner picks the group). Trade-off: meets owner demand; brings the exposure and the review/moderation load Yelp had to build.
- C — Defer to after launch; watch whether owners write identity words in values tags. Trade-off: no build or moderation cost now; data informs the call.
- Lean: C for launch, keep A/B open. Your call.

Related open point: can a *badge* (not the tag) be filtered or searched? The rule covers values; badges are not stated. Matters most if B is chosen.

Not proposed as categories: product type (#glutenfree, #linen, #coffee — regular tags), politics/advocacy (Ethical Consumer's "politics" area — divisive, not a practice), owner identity (see §2 question).

Notes:
- 7–10 are four eco badges. That mirrors how specific the wild gets (Yelp, Etsy) and the FTC's push away from vague "eco-friendly." An alternative is one "Earth-friendly" badge with these as sub-lines — but that is exactly the vague umbrella the FTC warns about.
- "Locally owned" can borrow AMIBA's test (≥50% owned by area residents, local decision-making, limited locations) as the help text.
- #lgbtqfriendly under "Everyone welcome" is a practice (welcoming), not ownership — but it can draw the same 2023-style targeting. Flag for the §2 decision.

## 4. Risks and lessons from the wild

1. **Vague green badges invite greenwashing — and legal risk.** FTC says "eco-friendly" reads as "good in every way." Amazon's single umbrella badge drew greenwashing criticism. Use specific badge names; keep "Says …" wording; never show a badge that implies a certification the owner didn't claim.
2. **Badges imply more than they say.** Animal-welfare buyers were ~20% *more* likely to believe false things about the label they bought. One-line badge meanings, shown on tap, are the fix.
3. **Visible identity draws lower ratings and hate.** Black-owned tag → lower ratings from aware reviewers; anti-Asian hate reviews 50× in a year; coordinated anti-LGBTQ+ campaigns. Yelp had to pre-screen reviews on tagged pages. Any identity or "friendly" badge needs a moderation plan before launch.
4. **Gaming and a low bar kill trust for everyone.** Nespresso's B Corp led Dr. Bronner's to quit. Self-reported badges with no way to challenge them invite the same. Options: member "doesn't match my visit" report, owner can be asked what's behind a badge (Etsy reserves the right to ask for receipts).
5. **Values are a tie-breaker, not the main reason people buy.** Quality 69% and price 63% vs sustainability 10% (top-3). Keep badges small; don't let them crowd price, hours, and what's for sale. Too many badges = noise; Faire (6) and Yelp eco (7) show short lists work.
