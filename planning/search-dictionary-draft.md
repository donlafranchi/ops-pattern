> **This is now the seed tag vocabulary, not a category-organised dictionary** *(2026-09-13)*. Categories are retired; **tags are the only vocabulary**, and what Don writes here is the set of tags that exist on day one. **The twelve headings below are scaffolding for reading, not structure** — they group the draft so it can be reviewed in sittings, and they do not survive into the app. **It is editable now**; the earlier hold, which waited on a category count, is lifted because there is no count to wait for.

**Don authors this; this is a draft so he edits rather than starts blank.** Seeded with farmers market items per his instruction. **Every term below is a candidate tag.** The headings are a reading aid only.

**This file is temporary.** When Don has edited it, the list moves into the app as a named constant beside `PAGE_CATEGORIES` and **this file is deleted** — a vocabulary read by code does not also live in a document that can drift from it.

**How to read it:** each term is a tag a creator might pick, and also a word a finder might type. **One list does both jobs** — the tag vocabulary and the search dictionary are the same thing, which is the whole point of the 2026-09-13 ruling. Terms are lowercase and matched case-insensitively.

## Notes for the edit

- **Cut freely.** A term nobody in this metro would type is worse than a missing one — it makes search look like it matched something arbitrary.
- **Local words beat correct words.** Whatever people here actually call a thing wins over the dictionary spelling.
- **Spellings are British-English in places** (yoghurt, jewellery, neighbourhood, colour). **Normalise to whichever the app uses**, or carry both — a member typing *jewelry* and getting nothing is the failure this exists to prevent.
- **Multi-word terms are allowed** and matched as a phrase: *"homemade soap"*, *"bike repair"*, *"run club"*.
- **A term may map to more than one category** — nothing here forbids it, and *"flowers"* arguably belongs to both Growing and Home & Body. Left single-mapped in this draft; say if that is wrong.
- **Roughly 400 terms as drafted.** If that is too many to review, the first two categories alone would carry a launch.
